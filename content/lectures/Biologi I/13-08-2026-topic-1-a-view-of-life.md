# Topic 1: A View of Life

**Biology** is the scientific study of life.

- There is **great diversity** among living things.
- Yet living things are:
  - Composed of the **same chemical elements** as non-living things.
  - Governed by the **same physical and chemical laws** that rule everything in the universe.
- There are **six characteristics** that define a living organism.

> **The 6 characteristics of life:** (1) Organized, (2) Require materials and energy, (3) Maintain homeostasis, (4) Respond to stimuli, (5) Reproduce and develop, (6) Have adaptations.

---

## 1. Living things are organized

- The levels of biological organization range from **atoms** all the way up to the **biosphere**.
- The **cell** is the basic unit of structure and function of all living things.
  - Organisms can be **unicellular** (one cell) or **multicellular** (many cells).
- Each level of organization is **more complex** than the level before it.
  - As complexity increases, each level gains **emergent properties** — new, unique characteristics that the simpler level did not have.

### Levels of biological organization

| Level | What it is |
| ----- | ---------- |
| **Atom** | Smallest unit of an element; made of electrons, protons, and neutrons |
| **Molecule** | Union of two or more atoms of the same or different elements |
| **Cell** | The structural and functional unit of all living things |
| **Tissue** | A group of cells with a common structure and function |
| **Organ** | Tissues functioning together for a specific task |
| **Organ system** | Several organs working together |
| **Organism** | An individual; complex individuals contain organ systems |
| **Population** | Organisms of the **same species** in a particular area |
| **Community** | Interacting **populations** in a particular area |
| **Ecosystem** | A community **plus** the physical environment |
| **Biosphere** | Regions of the Earth's crust, waters, and atmosphere inhabited by living things |

## 2. Life requires materials and energy

- **Energy** is the capacity to do work.
  - Energy is needed to maintain organization and carry out life-sustaining processes such as chemical reactions.
  - **Metabolism** is all the chemical reactions that occur in a cell.
- The **Sun** is the **ultimate source of energy** for nearly all life on Earth.
  - Plants, algae, and some other organisms capture solar energy and perform **photosynthesis**.
  - **Photosynthesis** converts solar energy into the **chemical energy of carbohydrates**.

### Chemical cycling and energy flow

Ecosystems are characterized by **chemical cycling** and **energy flow**:

- Chemicals are **not used up** when organisms die.
- Chemicals **move** from one population to another **in a food chain**.
  - Example path: **producers → consumers → decomposers**.
- Through **death and decomposition**, chemicals are **returned to living plants** (recycled).
- Energy from the Sun **flows through** plants and the rest of the food chain as one population feeds on another.
- Because energy flows one way (and is lost as heat), there must be a **constant input of solar energy**.

---

## 3. Living things maintain homeostasis

- **Homeostasis** is the maintenance of internal conditions within certain boundaries.
  - It is essential that an organism **maintains a state of biological balance**.
- **Feedback systems** monitor internal conditions and make adjustments.
  - Example (**negative feedback**): when body temperature **rises**, cooling is activated (**sweating**); when body temperature **falls**, heating is activated (**shivering**).

## 4. Living things respond to stimuli

- Living things **interact** with the environment and **respond to changes** in it.
- The ability to respond often produces **movement**.
  - e.g. a plant bending toward the Sun to capture light; a turtle withdrawing into its shell for safety; birds migrating in response to environmental change.
- Responses help **ensure the survival** of the organism and allow it to carry out its biological activities.

---

## 5. Living things reproduce and develop

- All living organisms must **reproduce** to maintain a population.
- **Reproduction** is the ability of every organism to give rise to **another organism like itself**.
- The **manner** of reproduction varies among different organisms.
- When organisms reproduce, they pass on copies of their **genetic information (genes)** to the next generation.
  - **Genes determine the characteristics** of an organism.
  - Example (humans): an egg (23 chromosomes) is fertilized by a sperm (23 chromosomes) to form a **zygote** with **46 chromosomes in 23 pairs**.

---

## 6. Living things have adaptations

- An **adaptation** is any modification that makes an organism **better able to function** in a particular environment.
- The **diversity of life** exists because, over long periods of time, **organisms respond to changing environments by developing new adaptations**.
- **Evolution** is the change in a population of organisms over time to become **more suited** to the environment.

> **Example — the emperor penguin's adaptations:** flipper-like wings for swimming, webbed feet with claws, a dense layer for warmth, and a shape suited to the cold — each feature helps it survive in its environment.

---

## Quick recap

- **Biology** = the study of life; life obeys the same chemical and physical laws as non-living matter.
- The **6 characteristics of life**: organized · needs materials & energy · homeostasis · responds to stimuli · reproduces & develops · has adaptations.
- **Organization** runs atom → molecule → cell → tissue → organ → organ system → organism → population → community → ecosystem → biosphere, each with **emergent properties**.
- Ecosystems **cycle chemicals** but **energy flows** one way, so life needs a **constant input of solar energy**.
- **Homeostasis** keeps internal conditions balanced through **feedback systems**.
- Organisms **reproduce**, passing on **genes**, and **adapt** over time through **evolution**.
