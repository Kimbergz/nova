## 3.1 Cell: The Basic Unit of Life

**All organisms are composed of cells.**

### The Cell Theory
Originated from the work of **Matthias Schleiden** (German botanist, **1838**) and **Theodor Schwann** (German zoologist, **1839**), who reasoned from microscopic observations that all plants and animals consist of cells.

- **Rudolf Virchow (1855):** new cells form through the **division of existing cells**.
- **August Weisman (1880):** the ancestry of all cells today can be traced from the basic similarities in their structures and functions.

> **The Cell Theory:**
> 1. Cells are the basic units of **structure/organization** and **function** in organisms.
> 2. All cells come from **pre-existing cells**, because cells are self-reproducing.

### Cell size
- Although cell sizes vary, they are mostly **microscopic** and must be measured in very small units.
- **1 metre = 1000 millimetres (mm) · 1 mm = 1000 micrometres (µm) · 1 µm = 1000 nanometres (nm).**
- Tools by scale: **electron microscope** and **super-resolution microscopy** (smallest), **light microscope**, then the **human eye**.

**Fun facts:** some cells move around the body (blood cells); others stay attached in one place (muscle cells); some divide and reproduce quickly (skin cells); some do not normally divide (nerve cells).

## 3.2 Prokaryotic & Eukaryotic Cells

### The Prokaryotes
- **Lack a membrane-bound nucleus** ("prokaryote" = *before the nucleus*) → **simpler** than a eukaryote.
- Placed into **two taxonomic domains:**

| | Archaea | Bacteria |
| - | ------- | -------- |
| **Cell type** | Prokaryote | Prokaryote |
| **Number of cells** | Unicellular | Unicellular |
| **Cell wall** | **Lacks** peptidoglycan | **Has** peptidoglycan |
| **Fun fact** | Live in extreme, harsh conditions | Most diverse and widespread group |

![Structure of a prokaryotic (bacterial) cell](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Average_prokaryote_cell-_en.svg?width=520)
*Image: "Average prokaryote cell" by Mariana Ruiz (LadyofHats), Wikimedia Commons (public domain).*

**Structure of prokaryotes — the cell envelope includes:**
- **Plasma membrane** — lipid bilayer with embedded and peripheral proteins. Can form internal pouches (**mesosomes**) that increase surface area.
- **Cell wall** — maintains shape; strengthened by **peptidoglycan**.
- **Glycocalyx** — a layer of polysaccharides.

**The prokaryotic cytoplasm** — a semifluid solution encased by the plasma membrane; contains water, inorganic & organic molecules, and enzymes.
- **Nucleoid** — region containing the single, circular DNA molecule.
- **Plasmids** — small accessory (extrachromosomal) rings of DNA.
- **Ribosomes** — tiny structures that synthesize proteins.

**External structures:**
- **Flagella** — provide motility.
- **Fimbriae** — small, bristle-like fibres that sprout from the cell surface.
- **Conjugation pili** — rigid tubular structures used to pass DNA from cell to cell (singular: pilus).

### The Eukaryotes
1. **Membrane-bound nucleus** that houses DNA.
2. Specialized and **compartmentalized organelles** that perform specific functions and isolate reactions from one another.
3. **Plasma membrane** separates cell contents from the environment, regulates passage of materials, and is a phospholipid bilayer with embedded proteins.
4. The eukaryotic cell has its own **control center**, **internal transport system**, **power plants**, **factories** for making materials, **packaging plants**, and even a **self-destruct system**.

> **Main difference:** prokaryotic cells **lack a nucleus and membrane-bound organelles**. Eukaryotes also have a supporting framework (**cytoskeleton**) to maintain shape and transport materials.

### Origin of organelles
1. **Origin of the nucleus** — **invagination** of the plasma membrane led to internal structures such as the nucleus.
2. **Origin of the endomembrane system** — **proliferation** of the membrane formed the endomembrane system, including the **endoplasmic reticulum (ER)** and **Golgi apparatus**.
3. **Endosymbiotic theory (energy-related organelles)** — **mitochondria** and **chloroplasts** originated from smaller prokaryotic cells that were **engulfed** by a larger eukaryotic ancestor. The host benefited from their ability to use oxygen (mitochondria) or perform photosynthesis (chloroplasts); over time they lived cooperatively inside the host.

**Evidence supporting the endosymbiotic theory:**
- Mitochondria and chloroplasts are **similar to bacteria** in size and structure.
- Both are surrounded by a **double membrane**.
- Both contain a limited amount of **genetic material** and divide by **splitting**.
- Both contain their **own ribosomes** and make some of their own proteins.
- The **RNA base sequence** of their ribosomes suggests a prokaryotic origin.

## 3.3 The Nucleus & Ribosomes

### The Nucleus
- The **command center** of the cell, usually near the center.
- **Nuclear envelope** — a **double membrane** that separates the nuclear contents from the cytoplasm.
- **Nuclear pores** — permit exchange between the **nucleoplasm** and cytoplasm.
- Contains **chromatin** in the semifluid nucleoplasm.
  - **Chromatin** — DNA associated with RNA and certain proteins.
  - **Chromosomes** — formed during cell division; carriers of genetic information.
- **Nucleolus** — most nuclei have one; it synthesizes **rRNA**.

### The Ribosomes
- **Site of protein synthesis** in the cell; composed of **rRNA**.
- Consist of a **large subunit** and a **small subunit** (both made in the nucleolus).
- Contain enzymes that form **peptide bonds**.
- May be located **on the ER** (making it "rough") or **free in the cytoplasm**, singly or in groups called **polyribosomes**.

## 3.4 Membranous Organelles in the Cytoplasm

There are **two classes** of organelles:
- **Endomembrane system** — organelles that communicate via membrane channels and small vesicles: **nuclear membrane, endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi apparatus, lysosomes, vesicles**, etc.
- **Energy-related organelles** — **mitochondria** and **chloroplasts**; independent and self-sufficient.

![Animal cell structure and organelles](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Animal_cell_structure_en.svg?width=560)
*Image: "Animal cell structure" by Mariana Ruiz (LadyofHats), Wikimedia Commons (public domain).*

![Plant cell structure and organelles](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Plant_cell_structure-en.svg?width=560)
*Image: "Plant cell structure" by Mariana Ruiz (LadyofHats), Wikimedia Commons (public domain).*

### The Endomembrane System
A network of organelles exchanging materials through small membrane-enclosed **transport vesicles**. Each vesicle has membrane proteins that act as **routing signals** to guide it to the correct destination.

**Endoplasmic Reticulum (ER)** — a system of membrane channels and saccules (flattened vesicles) **continuous with the nuclear envelope**.

| Rough ER | Smooth ER |
| -------- | --------- |
| **Studded with ribosomes** on the cytoplasmic side | **No ribosomes** |
| Synthesizes **proteins** | Synthesizes **lipids** (phospholipids and cholesterol to make cell membrane) |
| Adds sugar to protein → **glycoproteins** | Breaks down **toxins** |
| Forms transport vesicles (→ Golgi) | Forms transport vesicles |

**Golgi Complex**
- Consists of stacks of flattened membranous sacs called **cisternae**.
- **Processes, sorts, and modifies** proteins.
- Each stack has three areas: the **cis face** (entry surface), the **trans face** (exit surface), and the **medial region** (in between).
- Pathway: a transport vesicle brings glycoprotein to the Golgi (**cis face**) → glycoprotein is further modified → at the **trans face** it is packaged into transport vesicles → transported to the **plasma membrane** (exocytosis).

**Lysosomes**
- **Membrane-bound vesicles** in the cytoplasm of animal cells.
- Fuse with incoming vesicles and **digest macromolecules** taken into the cell.
- Contain **powerful digestive enzymes** and are **highly acidic**.
- Digest large molecules into simpler subunits.

> **Did you know?** Specialized cells may have more or fewer of an organelle depending on their function — e.g. **liver cells** (which detoxify drugs) have more **smooth ER**; **nerve cells** (which carry impulses) have more **plasma membrane**.

### Vacuoles
- Plant vacuoles are like lysosomes — enclosed membranous sacs.
- Plant cells have a **central vacuole** (up to **90 %** of the cell's volume); significant for plant growth and development.
- **Functions:** storage of water, nutrients, pigments and waste products; development of **turgor pressure** providing structural support.

### Microbodies — example: Peroxisomes
Contain specialized enzymes to perform specific functions. Peroxisomes:
- Transfer hydrogen from other compounds to **oxygen**.
- Break down **fatty acid** molecules.
- Synthesize **phospholipids**.
- Degrade **alcohol** in yeast cells; **detoxify** toxic compounds in human liver and kidney cells.
- Convert stored **fats to sugars** in plant seeds.

### Energy-Producing Organelles
Mitochondria and chloroplasts are **energy-converting** organelles: they convert energy from one form to another (chemical or light energy → **ATP**), have their own **ribosomes and DNA**, and support the **theory of serial endosymbiosis**.

**Mitochondria**
- Surrounded by a **double membrane**.
  - **Outer membrane** is smooth, allowing small molecules through.
  - **Inner membrane** strictly regulates what crosses it; it surrounds the **matrix** and is folded (convoluted) to form **cristae**.
- Produce **most of the ATP** used by the cell.

![Structure of a mitochondrion](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Animal_mitochondrion_diagram_en_%28edit%29.svg?width=520)
*Image: "Animal mitochondrion diagram" by Mariana Ruiz (LadyofHats), Wikimedia Commons (public domain).*

**Chloroplast**
- Bounded by a **double membrane**; a type of **plastid** and the site of **photosynthesis**.
- Inner membrane is folded into disc-like **thylakoids**, which stack to form **grana**, suspended in the semi-fluid **stroma**.
- **Green** due to **chlorophyll**, found **only** in the inner membranes.
- Photosynthesis: synthesizes carbohydrates from **CO₂ and H₂O**, converting energy-poor compounds into energy-rich ones:
  - **solar energy + carbon dioxide + water → carbohydrate + oxygen**
- Chloroplasts make carbohydrates used by mitochondria; the mitochondria in turn produce CO₂ and H₂O used by chloroplasts.

## 3.5 The Cytoskeleton

The cytoskeleton contains **microfilaments**, **intermediate filaments**, and **microtubules**.

**Functions:** maintain cell shape · assist movement of the cell and organelles · make internal transport possible. It is **dynamic** — it can rearrange its protein components in response to internal and external changes.

| Component | Made of | Role |
| --------- | ------- | ---- |
| **Microfilaments** (7 nm) | Actin | **Muscular contraction** (major contractile component); animal **cell division** |
| **Microtubules** (25 nm) | Tubulin | Radiate from the **centrosome**; found in centrioles, cilia and flagella; act as an **internal transport system** for vesicles and organelles |
| **Intermediate filaments** (10 nm) | Keratin | Give great **mechanical strength** (e.g. to skin cells) |

## Summary — Prokaryotic vs Eukaryotic cells

| Feature | Prokaryotic (1–20 µm) | Animal (eukaryote) | Plant (eukaryote) |
| ------- | --------------------- | ------------------ | ----------------- |
| Cell wall | Usually (peptidoglycan) | No | Yes (cellulose) |
| Plasma membrane | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Nucleus | No | Yes | Yes |
| Nucleolus | No | Yes | Yes |
| Ribosomes | Yes (smaller) | Yes | Yes |
| Endoplasmic reticulum | No | Yes | Yes |
| Golgi apparatus | No | Yes | Yes |
| Lysosomes | No | Yes | Not usually |
| Mitochondria | No | Yes | Yes |
| Chloroplasts | No | No | Yes |
| Peroxisomes | No | Usually | Usually |
| Cytoskeleton | No | Yes | Yes |
| Centrioles | No | Yes | No |
