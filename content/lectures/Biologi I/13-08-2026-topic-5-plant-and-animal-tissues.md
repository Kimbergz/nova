**Outline:** 5.1 Tissues in Flowering Plants · 5.2 Tissues in Animals

## 5.1 Tissues in Flowering Plants

As a plant embryo begins to develop, the first cells are called **meristem cells**.
- **Meristem cells** — undifferentiated cells, able to **divide indefinitely** and give rise to many types of differentiated cells.

### The three types of meristem (meristematic) tissue

| | Apical meristem | Intercalary meristem | Lateral meristem (cambium) |
| - | --------------- | -------------------- | -------------------------- |
| **Location** | Apices (growing tips) of roots, main shoots, lateral shoots | Base of leaves; internodes (either side of nodes) | Lateral sides of stems and roots |
| **Function** | **Primary growth** — increases length/height | **Longitudinal growth**; adds primary tissues; rapid regrowth after grazing/cutting | **Secondary growth** — increases diameter (thickness) |
| **Key points** | Elongation of roots & shoots; forms primary tissues; vertical growth | Common in **monocots** (grasses, wheat); base of leaf in *Pinus*, nodes in Mint | Cells divide in one plane; present in **dicots**, absent in monocots; forms **annual rings**; types: vascular cambium, cork cambium |

### From apical meristem to the three tissue systems
Apical meristems produce three primary meristems, each giving rise to one specialized tissue:
- **Protoderm → Epidermal tissue** — forms the outer protective covering of the plant.
- **Ground meristem → Ground tissue** — fills the interior of the plant.
- **Procambium → Vascular tissue** — transports water and nutrients and provides support.

These make up the **three tissue systems** found throughout the plant body (in the leaf, stem, and root): **epidermal, ground, and vascular**.

### 1. Epidermal tissue
Forms the outer protective covering of a plant.
- In **herbaceous** plants, the epidermal tissue system is the **epidermis**.
- **Woody** plants first produce an epidermis, then **periderm replaces the epidermis** with the outer bark.

In older woody plants, the stem epidermis is replaced by **periderm (cork)** during secondary growth to increase the plant's girth:
- The major component of periderm is **cork cells**, made by the **cork cambium**.
- Periderm is **nonliving** (encrusted with **suberin**) — it protects the plant and resists fungal infection, bacteria, and animal attacks.
- **Lenticel** — an area where the cork cambium overproduces cork (causing ridges and cracks); it is the **site of gas exchange** between the stem interior and the air.

**Modifications of the epidermis:**
- Some are covered with a **waxy cuticle**.
- Root epidermal cells have **root hairs** — increase surface area for water absorption.
- Stems, leaves, and reproductive organs have **trichomes** — protect from too much sun and moisture loss, protect from herbivores (by producing toxic substances), remove excess salts in salty environments, and increase reflection of light to cool tissue in desert plants.
- The lower leaf surface contains **stomata** for gas exchange.

### 2. Ground tissue
- Forms the **bulk** of a flowering plant; consists of the cells between the dermal and vascular tissues.
- **Functions:** provides **support** and **storage** of materials.
- Composed of three cell types: **parenchyma, collenchyma, sclerenchyma**.

| Ground tissue | Key features | Function |
| ------------- | ------------ | -------- |
| **Parenchyma** | Least specialized, most abundant; found in all organs. Contain **chloroplast** (photosynthesis) and colourless plastids (store products); **large vacuole** stores starch, water, oils. Make up soft tissues (pith, cortex, leaf mesophyll). Can **divide** into more specialized cells (e.g. roots from a stem cutting in water — vegetative reproduction). | Photosynthesis & storage |
| **Collenchyma** | **Thicker primary walls** of cellulose; form bundles under the epidermis (elongated cells); **no chloroplast**; in parts still growing; near the surface of the cortex in stems, along leaf veins. | **Flexible support** to young parts; protection against breakage |
| **Sclerenchyma** | **Thick, lignified secondary wall** (hard, rigid); **lack cell contents** at maturity; no chloroplast. Two forms: **sclereids/stone cells** (gritty texture in pear) and **elongated fibers**. | Support **mature** regions; support vascular tissue; component of **wood** |

### 3. Vascular tissue
Two types: **xylem** and **phloem**.

**Xylem** — conducts **water and minerals** from roots to leaves; made of **dead cells with lignified walls**. Conducting cells:
- **Tracheids** — long with **tapered ends**; water moves across **pits** in the end and side walls.
- **Vessel elements** — larger, with **perforation plates** in their end walls; join to form a **continuous vessel** for water and mineral transport.

**Phloem** — transports **sucrose and other organic compounds** from leaves to roots. Conducting cells:
- **Sieve-tube members** — arranged end to end to form a **sieve tube**; each has a companion cell.
- **Companion cells** — connected to sieve-tube members by **plasmodesmata**; have a **nucleus** that controls and maintains both cells.
- **Sclerenchyma fibers** lend support to the phloem.

**Location of vascular tissue:** in the **root** → the vascular cylinder; in the **stem** → the vascular bundles; in **leaves** → the leaf veins.

![Comparison of monocot and dicot features](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Monocot_vs_Dicot.svg?width=520)
*Image: "Monocot vs Dicot," Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA).*

**Distribution of tissues — eudicot vs monocot stems:**
- **Eudicot:** vascular bundles arranged in a **ring** near the periphery; a central **pith** is present; ground tissue is separated into cortex and pith.
- **Monocot:** vascular bundles **scattered** throughout the ground tissue, with no distinct ring or pith; ground tissue is not clearly separated into cortex and pith.

**Distribution of tissues — eudicot vs monocot roots:**
- **Eudicot:** xylem often arranged in a **star shape**, with phloem between the arms of the star; pith is less prominent or absent.
- **Monocot:** xylem and phloem arranged in a **ring-like pattern**; a **prominent pith** is present.
- The three primary root tissues: **epidermis** (dermal), **cortex** (ground), and **stele** (vascular — including endodermis, pericycle, pith, xylem, phloem).

## 5.2 Tissues in Animals

**Tissue** — a collection of specialized cells of similar or different type that perform a **common function** in the body.

**Four types of animal tissue:**
1. **Epithelial** — covers body surfaces, lines body cavities, and forms glands.
2. **Connective** — binds and supports body parts.
3. **Muscular** — moves the body and its parts.
4. **Nervous** — receives stimuli and transmits nerve impulses.

### 1. Epithelial Tissue
**General characteristics:**
- **Tightly packed cells** forming a continuous layer.
- Cover surfaces and line body cavities.
- Often exposed to the environment on one side but attached on the other to a **basement membrane** (a thin layer of proteins anchoring the epithelium to the extracellular matrix).
- Has a **protective function** and can be modified for **secretion, absorption, excretion, and filtration**.

![Types of epithelial tissue](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Illu_epithelium.jpg?width=520)
*Image: "Illu epithelium," US National Cancer Institute via Wikimedia Commons (public domain).*

**Classification:**
- **Simple epithelia** — a single layer of cells; three cell shapes: **squamous** (flattened), **cuboidal** (cube-shaped), **columnar** (column-like).
- **Stratified epithelia** — more than one layer; only the bottom layer touches the basement membrane.
- **Pseudostratified epithelia** — appears layered, but true layers do not exist because **each cell touches** the basement membrane.
- **Transitional epithelia** — an intermediate form; shape changes in response to tension; can stretch and return. Location: **urinary bladder** (dome-shaped when relaxed; squamous when distended, allowing the bladder to expand without tearing).
- **Glandular epithelia** — secretes a product; a gland may be a single cell or many. **Exocrine glands** secrete into ducts; **endocrine glands** secrete internally and are transported by the bloodstream.

**Types of epithelium in vertebrates:**

| Type | Location | Function |
| ---- | -------- | -------- |
| Simple squamous | Lining of lungs, blood vessels | Allows diffusion |
| Simple cuboidal | Lining of kidney tubules, various glands | Absorbs molecules |
| Simple columnar | Lining of small intestine, uterine tubes | Absorbs nutrients |
| Pseudostratified ciliated columnar | Lining of trachea | Sweeps impurities toward the throat |
| Stratified squamous | Lining of nose, mouth, esophagus, anal canal, vagina | Protects |

### 2. Connective Tissue
- The **most abundant, widespread, and varied** tissue, with the widest range of functions: **connect, support, bind, separate** other tissues and organs.
- Has **three components:**
  1. **Specialized cells** — fibroblast, adipocytes, mast cell, chondrocytes, osteoblast.
  2. **Ground substance** — noncellular material; varies in texture (solid, fluid, semifluid).
  3. **Protein fibers** — **collagen** (strength & flexibility), **reticular** (thin, highly branched collagen), **elastic** (elastin; not as strong as collagen but more elastic).
- **Ground substance + fibers = connective tissue matrix.**
- **Functions:** structural framework; transporting fluid and dissolved materials; protecting delicate organs; supporting/surrounding/interconnecting tissues; storing energy reserves; defending the body from microorganisms.

Connective tissue is classified into **three major categories: Fibrous, Supportive, Fluid.**

**A) Fibrous tissue** — has **fibroblasts** (secrete fibres that maintain the framework); cells separated by a jelly-like matrix containing **white collagen fibres** and **yellow elastic fibres**.
- **1. Loose (areolar) fibrous tissue** — loosely arranged collagen and elastic fibres with abundant ground substance. *Location:* beneath the dermis, digestive/respiratory/urinary tracts, between muscles, around blood vessels, nerves and joints. *Functions:* protective covering over organs, allows organs to expand, supports epithelium and organs. Specialised cells: fibroblast, white blood cell, stem cell, adipose cell.
- **2. Adipose tissue** — few fibres (mostly reticular around the adipocytes), moderate ground substance. *Location:* under skin, around kidney, eyeballs, surface of heart, in breast. *Functions:* reserve food fuel, insulation against heat loss, supports & protects organs by cushioning. *Structure:* closely packed **adipocytes** (enlarged fibroblasts that store fat), with the nucleus pushed to the side by large fat droplets.
- **3. Dense fibrous tissue** — tightly packed collagenous fibres with a fine network of elastic fibres, less ground substance; strong, providing tensile strength and resistance to stretching. Two types:
  - **Ligaments** — connect **bone to bone** at joints; more stretchy, more elastic fibres, densely packed collagen.
  - **Tendons** — connect **muscle to bone**; less stretchy, fewer elastic fibres, less ordered collagen.
  - Both are **poorly vascularised** (few blood vessels) and have a **reduced capacity for healing**.

**B) Supportive tissue** — provides structure, shape, protection, and leverage for movement. Includes **cartilage** and **bone**.

**Cartilage** — a **non-vascularised** tissue; specialised cells (**chondrocytes**) sit in small chambers called **lacunae**, separated by a tissue matrix that is **solid but flexible**.

| Cartilage | Features | Location | Function |
| --------- | -------- | -------- | -------- |
| **Hyaline** | Very fine collagen fibres; chondrocytes widely dispersed | Nose, rings of respiratory passage (trachea & bronchi), human fetal skeleton | Support & flexibility; reduce friction for joint movement |
| **Elastic** | More elastic than collagen fibres (more flexible); chondrocytes in a threadlike network of elastic fibres | Outer ear, eustachian tube, epiglottis | Flexible surface that keeps its shape after bending |
| **Fibrocartilage** | **Strongest**; matrix with thick, strong collagenous fibres | Intervertebral discs, wedges in the knee joint | Strong support; high tensile strength; resistance to compression (**shock absorber**) |

**Bone**
- Ground substance contains a **hard matrix of inorganic salts** (mainly **calcium salts**) deposited around protein fibres (especially collagen).
  - **Calcium salts** → give rigidity.
  - **Protein fibres** → provide elasticity and strength.
- Bone cells (**osteocytes**) sit in **lacunae** and maintain the bone matrix.
- Lacunae are arranged in **concentric circles** within an **osteon**, around tiny tubes called the **central (Haversian) canal**.

![Structure of compact and spongy bone](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Illu_compact_spongy_bone.jpg?width=520)
*Image: "Compact and spongy bone," US National Cancer Institute via Wikimedia Commons (public domain).*

**C) Fluid tissue** — connective tissue located in blood and lymph vessels.
- **Blood** — connective tissue embedded in a **liquid matrix (plasma)**. *Functions:* transport nutrients and oxygen to cells, remove CO₂ and wastes, maintain ion and pH balance, distribute heat. Two cell types: **red blood cells (RBC)** carry oxygen; **white blood cells (WBC)** fight infection.
  - **Platelets** — the smallest, colourless, **non-nucleated** cells; form a plug that seals a damaged vessel and assist the **blood-clotting** process.
- **Lymph** — a fluid connective tissue in the lymphatic vessels. Absorbs interstitial fluid and returns it to the cardiovascular system; contains **lacteals** that absorb fat molecules from the small intestine; contains **lymphocytes** that remove foreign material via lymph nodes.

### 3. Muscular Tissue
Muscular tissue is composed of cells made of **muscle fibres**. Three types:

| | Skeletal | Cardiac | Smooth |
| - | -------- | ------- | ------ |
| **Location** | Attached to skeleton | Walls of the heart | Walls of stomach, intestines, blood vessels, uterus |
| **Control** | Voluntary | Involuntary | Involuntary |
| **Shape of fibres** | Elongated, cylindrical, blunt ends | Elongated, cylindrical, branch and fuse | Elongated, spindle-shaped, pointed ends |
| **Striations** | Present | Present | Absent |
| **Nuclei per fibre** | Many | One or two | One |
| **Position of nuclei** | Peripheral | Central | Central |
| **Speed of contraction** | Most rapid | Intermediate | Slowest |
| **Resistance to fatigue** | Least | Intermediate | Greatest |

*(Cardiac muscle fibres are joined by **intercalated discs**.)*

![The three types of muscle tissue: skeletal, smooth, cardiac](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/414_Skeletal_Smooth_Cardiac.jpg?width=560)
*Image: "Skeletal, Smooth and Cardiac muscle" by OpenStax College, Wikimedia Commons (CC BY 3.0).*

### 4. Nervous Tissue
The nervous system contains nerve cells called **neurons** and **neuroglia** (supporting cells).

**A neuron has three parts:**
- **Dendrites** — receive the stimulus and conduct signals to the cell body.
- **Cell body** — has most of the cytoplasm and contains the nucleus.
- **Axon** — conducts the nerve impulse away from the cell body; covered by the **myelin sheath**.

![Structure of a neuron](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Blausen_0657_MultipolarNeuron.png?width=520)
*Image: "Multipolar Neuron" by BruceBlaus, Wikimedia Commons (CC BY 3.0).*

**Neuroglia** — make up about **half the volume of the brain**; they **support and nourish** neurons. Several types:
- **Microglia** — support neurons and **phagocytize** bacteria and cellular debris.
- **Astrocytes** — provide nutrients and produce a hormone known as cell-derived growth factor.
- **Oligodendrocytes** — form **myelin** in the brain.

## Summary

**Plant tissues:** meristem (apical → primary growth, intercalary → longitudinal growth, lateral → secondary growth) produce three tissue systems — **epidermal** (protection), **ground** (parenchyma/collenchyma/sclerenchyma → support & storage), and **vascular** (xylem = water up; phloem = sugars down).

**Animal tissues:** **epithelial** (covers/lines/secretes), **connective** (fibrous, supportive [cartilage & bone], fluid [blood & lymph]), **muscular** (skeletal, cardiac, smooth), and **nervous** (neurons + neuroglia).
