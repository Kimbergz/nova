## 1. Mitochondria: The Powerhouse of the Cell

Besides producing ATP, mitochondria also: regulate cell death (apoptosis), help fight infections, produce signalling molecules, help build amino acids/lipids/nucleotides, and influence ageing.

A mitochondrion is bound by a **double membrane** (outer + inner) with an **intermembrane space** between them. The inner membrane invaginates to form shelf-like **cristae**.

| Region | Location of |
|---|---|
| Cristae (inner membrane folds) | Electron transport chain (ETC) |
| Matrix (innermost space) | Prep reaction & citric acid cycle |

## 2. Overview of Cellular Respiration (8.1)

**Cellular respiration**: the process by which cells acquire energy (as ATP) by breaking down nutrient molecules produced by photosynthesizers.

**Aerobic respiration** — a form of cellular respiration that consumes O₂ and produces CO₂; occurs in the **mitochondria**. It involves the complete breakdown of glucose to CO₂ and H₂O:

C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + energy

- Energy is released **step-wise** (several phases), which allows ATP to be produced efficiently
- **Glucose is oxidized** (electrons removed) and **O₂ is reduced** (electrons + H⁺ combine with O₂ to form water)

### Coenzymes: NAD⁺ and FAD

Both are oxidation-reduction coenzymes — oxidized when they give up electrons, reduced when they accept electrons. Think of them as a **taxi**: they pick up electrons (and H⁺) at one stage, carry them, then drop them off at a later stage of respiration and return to be reloaded.

| Coenzyme | Accepts | Becomes |
|---|---|---|
| NAD⁺ (nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide) | 2 electrons + 1 H⁺ | NADH |
| FAD (flavin adenine dinucleotide) | 2 electrons + 2 H⁺ | FADH₂ |

### The Four Stages of Aerobic Cellular Respiration

| Stage | Location | Key products (per glucose) |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Glycolysis | Cytoplasm | glucose → 2 pyruvate; net **2 ATP**, 2 NADH |
| 2. Preparatory (prep) reaction | Mitochondrial matrix | 2 pyruvate → 2 acetyl-CoA; 2 NADH, 2 CO₂ |
| 3. Citric acid cycle (Krebs cycle) | Mitochondrial matrix | **2 ATP**, 6 NADH, 2 FADH₂, 4 CO₂ |
| 4. Electron transport chain (ETC) + chemiosmosis | Cristae | **32 or 34 ATP** by oxidative phosphorylation |

## 3. Outside the Mitochondria: Glycolysis (8.2)

Glycolysis occurs in the **cytoplasm**, outside the mitochondria. It does **not require oxygen directly** and is the **common pathway** for both aerobic and anaerobic respiration. It consists of **10 reactions**, each with its own enzyme, in two phases:

**1) Energy Investment Phase** — 2 ATP are used to activate glucose, which splits into **two G3P** (glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate) molecules, each carrying a phosphate group from an ATP.

**2) Energy Capture Phase** — each G3P is oxidized (loses electrons + H⁺, picked up by NAD⁺ → 2 NADH total) and phosphorylated to 1,3-bisphosphoglycerate (BPG). BPG → 3-phosphoglycerate (3PG), producing ATP by **substrate-level phosphorylation**. Water is removed from 3PG to form phosphoenolpyruvate (PEP), which is converted to **pyruvate**, producing more ATP by substrate-level phosphorylation. Four ATP are produced in total in this phase — a **net gain of 2 ATP** per glucose (4 produced − 2 consumed).

**Substrate-level phosphorylation**: an enzyme directly transfers a high-energy phosphate group from a substrate to ADP, producing one ATP molecule (no membrane/electron transport involved). In glycolysis this happens twice per G3P: BPG → 3PG, and PEP → pyruvate.

*Figure 2: Summary of glycolysis — 2 ATP invested to split glucose, then the payoff phase (doubled, since glucose splits into two 3-carbon molecules) yields 4 ATP and 2 NADH, for a net gain of 2 ATP and 2 NADH per glucose. **Inputs and outputs of glycolysis (per glucose):**

| Inputs | Outputs |
|---|---|
| 1 (6C) glucose, 2 NAD⁺, 2 ATP, 4 ADP + 4 Pᵢ | 2 (3C) pyruvate, 2 NADH, 2 ADP, 4 ATP total → **net gain 2 ATP** |

## 4. Outside the Mitochondria: Fermentation (8.3)

Pyruvate is a pivotal metabolite: if **O₂ is available**, pyruvate enters the mitochondria for aerobic respiration (yielding ~34 more ATP). If **O₂ is not available**, **fermentation** — an anaerobic process — occurs in the cytoplasm: glucose is *incompletely* metabolized to lactate, or to CO₂ + alcohol (depending on the organism).

**Fermentation** reduces pyruvate to either lactate or alcohol + CO₂. Its key role is that **NADH transfers its electrons to pyruvate and returns to glycolysis as NAD⁺**, regenerating the NAD⁺ supply so glycolysis (and ATP production) can continue.

| | Alcoholic fermentation | Lactic acid fermentation |
|---|---|---|
| Carried out by | Yeasts and plants | Certain bacteria, fungi and animals |
| Produces | CO₂ + ethyl alcohol | Lactic acid (lactate) |
| Commercial use | Alcoholic beverages, bread | Cheese, yogurt, kimchi |

**In humans**: during strenuous exercise, the respiratory/cardiovascular systems can't deliver O₂ to muscle cells fast enough for aerobic respiration, so muscle cells switch to **lactic acid fermentation** to keep producing ATP. As lactic acid accumulates in the muscle cells, it is associated with **muscle fatigue and pain**.

**Efficiency**: fermentation yields 2 ATP/glucose ≈ 14.6 kcal, vs. 686 kcal available from complete oxidation of glucose → only **2.1% efficient**. Only 2 ATP/glucose vs. 36–38 ATP/glucose from full cellular respiration.

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| ATP production without oxygen | Very low ATP yield |
| Quick burst of ATP for muscular activity | End products can be toxic (lactic acid → cramps/low pH; ethanol toxic at high conc.) |
| Regenerates NAD⁺, letting glycolysis continue | Incomplete breakdown of glucose |
| Useful in food & beverage industries | Not sustainable long-term |

**Inputs and outputs of fermentation:**

| Inputs | Outputs |
|---|---|
| Glucose, 2 ADP + 2 Pᵢ | 2 lactate OR (2 alcohol + 2 CO₂); **2 ATP net gain** |

## 5. Inside the Mitochondria (8.4)

Three processes occur inside the mitochondria: **(1) Prep reaction, (2) Citric acid cycle, (3) Electron transport chain**.

### 5.1 Preparatory (Prep) Reaction — the "link" reaction

Connects glycolysis to the citric acid cycle. Pyruvate enters the mitochondrial matrix (when O₂ is present) and is converted: **pyruvate (3C) → acetyl-CoA (2C)**. Occurs **twice per glucose molecule** (once per pyruvate):

1. One carbon is removed as CO₂, oxidizing pyruvate into an acetyl group (2C)
2. NAD⁺ is reduced by accepting electrons, producing NADH
3. The acetyl group attaches to Coenzyme A (CoA) to form acetyl-CoA
4. CO₂ is released and transported out into the cytoplasm

| Inputs | Outputs |
|---|---|
| 2 (3C) pyruvate, 2 NAD⁺ | 2 (2C) acetyl-CoA, 2 NADH, 2 CO₂ |

### 5.2 Citric Acid Cycle (Krebs Cycle)

A cyclic pathway of 8 reactions occurring in the mitochondrial matrix. Turns **twice per glucose** (once for each pyruvate/acetyl-CoA).

1. Acetyl-CoA (2C) + oxaloacetate (4C) → citrate (6C)
2. Citrate → isomerized to isocitrate (6C)
3–4. Isocitrate is oxidized and undergoes decarboxylation twice: → α-ketoglutarate (5C) → succinyl-CoA (4C); **2 NADH and 2 CO₂ produced**
5. Succinyl-CoA → succinate (4C); **ATP produced by substrate-level phosphorylation** (via GTP)
6. Succinate → fumarate (4C); **FADH₂ produced**
7. Fumarate → malate (4C)
8. Malate → oxaloacetate (4C), regenerating the cycle; **NADH produced**

**Importance**: generates NADH and FADH₂ (electron carriers for the ETC) and produces ATP directly by substrate-level phosphorylation.

**Inputs and outputs of the citric acid cycle (per glucose, i.e. 2 turns):**

| Inputs | Outputs |
|---|---|
| 2 acetyl groups, 6 NAD⁺, 2 FAD, 2 ADP + 2 Pᵢ | 4 CO₂, 6 NADH, 2 FADH₂, 2 ATP |

### 5.3 Electron Transport Chain (ETC) and Chemiosmosis

The ETC occurs in the **cristae** of the mitochondria. It is a series of carrier molecules (protein/cytochrome complexes) that receive electrons from NADH and FADH₂, transfer them sequentially to lower energy states, and use the released energy to **pump H⁺** across the inner membrane, generating an electrochemical gradient. ATP is produced by **oxidative phosphorylation** (ETC + chemiosmosis).

**Electron transport chain steps:**
1. NADH is oxidized to NAD⁺, giving up 2 e⁻; H⁺ is pumped through **NADH-Q reductase**
2. **Coenzyme Q** carries the electrons to **Cytochrome reductase**. FADH₂ can also be oxidized to FAD, passing its electrons to Coenzyme Q and releasing H⁺
3. Cytochrome reductase accepts the electrons, pumps H⁺, and passes electrons to **Cytochrome C**
4. Cytochrome C carries the electrons to **Cytochrome oxidase**
5. Cytochrome oxidase accepts the electrons and pumps H⁺; finally, **O₂ accepts the electrons** and combines with H⁺ to form **H₂O**

**Chemiosmosis:**
6. A high concentration of H⁺ builds up in the intermembrane space vs. the matrix, creating an electrochemical gradient
7. **ATP synthase** allows H⁺ to flow back down its gradient into the matrix; this flow drives the synthesis of ATP from ADP + Pᵢ
8. ATP moves out of the mitochondria through an ATP channel protein to be used for cellular work

**ETC outcomes:**
- **Oxygen**: the final electron acceptor; combines with H⁺ to form water
- **Hydrogen**: H⁺ from NADH deliver enough energy to synthesise **3 ATP**; H⁺ from FADH₂ only enough for **2 ATP** (FADH₂ enters the chain "later" than NADH, bypassing the first H⁺-pumping step)
- **Coenzyme**: NAD⁺/FAD are reused (recycled), increasing cellular efficiency since the cell doesn't need to keep synthesising new coenzymes

**Real-world link — cyanide poisoning**: cyanide (CN⁻) blocks **Complex IV (cytochrome c oxidase)**, preventing electrons from being passed to O₂. This halts the ETC entirely, causing a severe ATP deficit, cellular hypoxia and rapid multi-organ failure (historically used in WWII gas chambers; fatal within minutes to victims). Symptoms include headache, nausea, dizziness, breathlessness, collapse and loss of consciousness.

## 6. Summary: Total ATP Yield per Glucose Molecule

| Stage | ATP (direct) | NADH → ETC ATP | FADH₂ → ETC ATP |
|---|---|---|---|
| Glycolysis | 2 (net) | 2 NADH → 4 or 6 ATP | — |
| Prep reaction | — | 2 NADH → 6 ATP | — |
| Citric acid cycle | 2 | 6 NADH → 18 ATP | 2 FADH₂ → 4 ATP |
| **Subtotal** | **4** | | **32 or 34** |
| **Total** | | **36 or 38 ATP** | |

**Why 32 or 34 (not a fixed number)?** Glycolysis's 2 NADH are made in the cytoplasm and must be *shuttled* into the mitochondria for the ETC — different cells use different shuttle mechanisms of varying efficiency to transport NADH/FADH₂ (and pyruvate, ADP) across the mitochondrial membrane. This shuttling costs energy, which can reduce the net ATP yield — hence some cells net 36 ATP and others 38 ATP per glucose.

**Overall pathway summary**: Glucose → (glycolysis: energy investment + energy capture) → Pyruvate + NADH + (net) ATP. Pyruvate then branches: if O₂ is unavailable → **fermentation** → lactic acid (lactic acid fermentation) or CO₂ + alcohol (alcoholic fermentation); if O₂ is available → **cellular respiration** (prep reaction → citric acid cycle → electron transport chain), producing far more ATP.
