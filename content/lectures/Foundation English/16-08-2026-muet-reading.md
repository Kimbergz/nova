# MUET Reading (Paper 800/3)

> **Note on accuracy:** secondary sources disagree slightly on the exact total question count for this paper (some say 40, some counts derived from part-by-part breakdowns total slightly higher). MPM does revise minor details between sittings. Treat the structure and question *types* below as reliable, but verify the exact current question count against the latest official MPM specimen paper close to your exam date.

## 1. The format at a glance

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| Duration | 75 minutes |
| Total marks | 90 (25% of final grade) |
| Number of parts | 7 |
| Number of texts | 10 |
| Question types | Multiple matching, 3-option MCQ, 4-option MCQ, gapped text |

### Part-by-part breakdown

| Part | Text(s) | Approx. length | Questions | Format |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 3 short texts (notices, ads, labels) | 100–150 words each | 4 | Multiple matching |
| 2 | 1 text | 300–400 words | 7 | MCQ, 3 options |
| 3 | 1 text | 300–400 words | 7 | MCQ, 3 options |
| 4 | 2 texts (compare/contrast) | Varies | 6 | MCQ, 3 options |
| 5 | 1 text with 6 sentences removed | 500–600 words | 6 | Gapped text (choose the missing sentence from a bank) |
| 6 | 1 longer text | 700–900 words | 7 | MCQ, 4 options |
| 7 | 1 longer text | 700–900 words | 7 | MCQ, 4 options |

Note the pattern: **texts get longer and questions get harder to skim as you progress** — Parts 1–4 reward fast, literal comprehension; Parts 5–7 reward genuine reading skill (inference, structure, tone). Budget your time accordingly (see §4).

## 2. What each part is actually testing

- **Part 1** — locating specific facts across short, real-world texts (notices, timetables, adverts). This is scanning, pure and simple.
- **Parts 2–3** — main idea and detail comprehension, plus basic inference ("what can be concluded from…").
- **Part 4** — comparing and contrasting information across two related texts; you must track *which text said what*, similar to the Listening Part 3 trap.
- **Part 5** — understanding how a text is logically structured: cause/effect, sequencing, and cohesion (how one sentence connects to the next via pronouns, linking words, and topic continuity).
- **Parts 6–7** — the deepest reading skills: tone, attitude, implication, writer's purpose, and detail — the 4-option MCQs here are deliberately harder to eliminate than the 3-option ones earlier in the paper.

## 3. Core techniques by question type

### Multiple matching (Part 1)
- Read the questions/statements first, then scan (don't read word-for-word) each short text for matching keywords or their synonyms.
- Watch for texts that answer *more than one* question, and questions that require combining information from two short texts.

### Multiple-choice, 3 & 4 options (Parts 2, 3, 4, 6, 7)
- Read the question stem before the passage in detail — know what you're hunting for.
- Eliminate options that are **true according to the text but don't answer the specific question asked** — this is the most common 4-option MCQ trap in Parts 6–7.
- Beware options that use exact words from the passage but distort the meaning ("word-lifting" traps) — MUET, like most English exams, often makes the correct answer a paraphrase rather than a verbatim lift.

### Gapped text (Part 5)
1. Read the base text first, ignoring the gaps, to understand the overall flow and topic.
2. At each gap, look at what comes immediately **before and after** — the missing sentence must connect grammatically and logically to both.
3. Use cohesion clues in the answer options: pronouns ("this," "it," "they") must refer back to something already mentioned; linking words ("however," "as a result," "in addition") signal the logical relationship needed.
4. Work through easier gaps first and use process of elimination — one leftover option and one leftover gap will often confirm each other at the end.

## 4. Time management (75 minutes, ~90 marks)

A reasonable budget, front-loading the easier/shorter parts and protecting time for the harder ones:

| Parts | Suggested time |
|---|---|
| 1 (short texts) | ~8 minutes |
| 2–3 (MCQ) | ~18 minutes |
| 4 (compare/contrast) | ~12 minutes |
| 5 (gapped text) | ~15 minutes |
| 6–7 (long texts, 4-option MCQ) | ~20 minutes |
| Buffer/review | ~2 minutes |

Adjust to your own strengths, but the principle holds: **don't let Part 1 eat time meant for Parts 6–7**, since the longer texts near the end carry equally weighted, often harder questions.

## 5. Worked example (gapped text logic)

**Base text excerpt:** *"Urban farming has grown rapidly in the last decade. ___(6)___ As a result, cities have started allocating unused land for community gardens."*

**Option bank includes:**
A) However, rural farming remains more profitable overall.
B) More residents have realised that growing their own food reduces both cost and carbon footprint.
C) Farmers have always preferred rural land for large-scale crops.

- The sentence after the gap begins with **"As a result"** — meaning the missing sentence must state a *cause* that leads to cities allocating land for gardens.
- Option A introduces a contrast ("However") that doesn't logically lead to "as a result" — discard.
- Option C is about rural farmers, unrelated to the urban-farming cause/effect chain — discard.
- **Correct answer: B** — it directly supplies the cause ("realised... reduces cost and carbon footprint") that the following "as a result" sentence logically continues.

This is the exact reasoning to apply at every gap: **read outward from the gap, not just the gap itself.**

## 6. Common mistakes that cost marks

- Reading every word of every passage at the same slow pace — Parts 1–4 reward skimming/scanning speed, not deep literary analysis.
- Choosing an option because it's factually true, without checking it actually answers the specific question asked.
- In Part 4, mixing up which of the two texts a detail came from.
- In Part 5, choosing a sentence that fits the topic generally but doesn't grammatically/logically bridge the specific gap (e.g. wrong pronoun reference).
- Running out of time on Parts 6–7 because too long was spent double-checking Part 1 or 2.
- Leaving questions blank — there's no penalty for guessing, so always fill something in before time runs out.

## 7. How to practise effectively

- Practise **skimming** (reading for gist/topic, fast) and **scanning** (hunting for a specific fact/number/name) as two distinct, deliberate skills, not just "reading faster."
- Read varied real-world English regularly — newspaper opinion pieces are excellent practice for Parts 6–7's tone/attitude/implication questions; notices and ads are good practice for Part 1.
- Build vocabulary through context and word parts (prefixes/suffixes) rather than isolated word-lists — MUET tests reading *in context*, not dictionary definitions.
- Time yourself on full past/specimen papers regularly in the final weeks, not just isolated parts, so you build stamina and pacing instinct for the full 75 minutes.

## 8. Quick pre-exam checklist

- Know which parts reward speed (1–4) versus which reward careful reasoning (5–7), and budget time accordingly.
- Comfortable with the gapped-text "read outward from the gap" technique.
- Practised distinguishing "true but doesn't answer the question" distractors from correct answers.
- Built a habit of skimming for gist before scanning for detail.
- Never leaving a question blank, even under time pressure.
