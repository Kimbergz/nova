# Topic 4: Landscape and Nursery Industry

**Outline:** 4.1 The Nursery Industry · 4.2 Herbs and Medicinal Plants · 4.3 Landscape Plants · 4.4 Turf Grass

> A **nursery** is where a plant's life begins — from a cutting, a bud, or a seed — before it is ever planted out. From there, plants branch into three big businesses: nurseries that multiply them, herb growers that turn them into medicine and health products, and landscapers who arrange them into the gardens, avenues, and golf courses we walk through every day.

---

## 4.1 The Nursery Industry

A **nursery** is an area where plants are raised for eventual planting out — comprising nursery beds, paths, and irrigated channels. It is a popular business investment in both urban and rural areas, and includes three core activities: **plant propagation**, **planting media preparation**, and **potting and re-potting**.

Commercial nurseries supply seedlings (orchids, fruit trees, oil palm, cocoa, coconut, ornamentals) to private estates, universities, government, or local councils. Commercial **orchid farms** often run their own nursery, propagating orchid seedlings by **tissue culture**.

### Types of nursery

| Type | Focus |
| ---- | ----- |
| **Fruit plant nursery** | Fruit plants regularly propagated and sold — e.g. air layering in citrus, grafting in sapodilla, other vegetative propagation |
| **Vegetable plant nursery** | Seasonal, annual and perennial crops (brinjal, tomato, gourds, onions) grown from seedlings; demand rising as buyers lose faith in chemically treated market vegetables |
| **Medicinal/aromatic plant nursery** | Plants used in place of allopathic medicine, and in foods and daily wear; valued for essential oils with health and aesthetic benefits |
| **Ornamental plant nursery** | Grown purely for decorative purposes; florists develop new flower forms that carry symbolic meaning as gifts |
| **Forest plant nursery** | A managed area producing tree seedlings under favourable conditions until ready for planting; ranges from small informal setups to large commercial enterprises |
| **Hi-tech nursery** | Uses protective structures — tissue culture laboratories, glasshouses/greenhouses, storage houses — instead of open-field cultivation |

### Facilities normally required

- **Shade house** — protects young seedlings from direct sunlight.
- **Mist propagation unit** — keeps humidity high for cuttings.
- **Planting media and potting area.**
- **Irrigation or fertigation unit.**
- **Fertiliser store.**
- **Storage area** for field equipment and agricultural chemicals (fungicides, insecticides, herbicides).

These facilities also support **plant propagation** (seeds, stem cuttings, bud-grafting, marcotting), **re-potting**, and **plant hardening**.

### Propagation methods

**1. Stem cutting** — a piece of stem is cut and grown into a new plant (e.g. Golden Duranta, Acalypha, Crotons, Rosemary, Thyme, Mint).

| Cutting type | Description | Leaf handling |
| ------------ | ----------- | -------------- |
| **Softwood cutting** | Green shoots; break easily when bent | No leaves discarded |
| **Semi-hardwood cutting** | Taken from a newly matured branch part | Lower leaves discarded |
| **Hardwood cutting** | Taken from a fully matured branch part; suitable for direct sowing into a polybag | All leaves discarded |

**2. Bud grafting** — a bud (scion) from one plant is inserted into a cut made on the stem of another (rootstock), e.g. using a **wedge cut**; used on Bougainvillea, Mango, and Hibiscus.

**3. Marcotting (air layering)** — roots are induced on a stem while it is still attached to the mother plant:
1. Remove a ring of bark from the chosen stem to expose the wood.
2. Wrap the wound with moist moss.
3. Cover the moss with plastic sheeting, sealed at both ends, until roots form inside.
4. Once rooted, cut the **marcot** away from the mother plant as a new, independent plant.

![Air layering (marcotting) being carried out on a tree branch](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Air-layering_on_a_avocado_tree_in_Goa%2C_India.jpg?width=600)

## 4.2 Herbs and Medicinal Plants

**Herbs** are plants with soft or woody, slender stems (erect or creeping) that contain active chemical compounds with medicinal properties, e.g. **antioxidants**. Only certain parts of the plant — or sometimes the whole plant — are used in traditional medicine.

- Used for traditional medicine, health products (drinks, capsules), or consumed as **"ulam"** (local salad).
- With advances in biotechnology, the global herb industry is forecast to be worth **RM60 billion a year**.

### Which plant part is used

| Plant part | Examples |
| ---------- | -------- |
| **Roots** | Ginseng (*Panax ginseng*), Tongkat ali (*Eurycoma longifolia*) |
| **Flower buds** | Torch ginger (*Etlingera elatior*) |
| **Fruits** | Noni (*Morinda citrifolia*), Bitter gourd (*Momordica charantia*) |
| **Leaf** | Pegaga/pennywort (*Centella asiatica*), Kacip fatimah (*Labisia pumila*), Misai kucing (*Orthosiphon aristatus*), Mas cotek (*Ficus deltoidea*) |
| **Bark/resins** | Gaharu (*Aquilaria malaccensis*) |

### Selected Malaysian medicinal plants

| Common name | Scientific name | Parts used | Traditional use |
| ----------- | ---------------- | ---------- | ---------------- |
| **Kacip Fatimah** | *Labisia pumila* | Whole herb | Aids childbirth, post-partum recovery, regulates menstrual cycle, eases menopausal symptoms |
| **Hempedu Bumi** | *Andrographis paniculata* | Leaves, whole plant | Analgesic, laxative, digestive; treats diabetes, fever, bronchitis, skin disease |
| **Senduduk** | *Melastoma malabathricum* | Leaves, shoots, roots | Cures diarrhoea; leaf/root paste prevents scars; root decoction relieves toothache |
| **Jerangau** | *Acorus calamus* | Rhizomes | Antibacterial, antiseptic; remedy for flatulence, colic, bronchial infection, asthma |
| **Devil's Cotton** | *Abroma augusta* | Roots, leaves | Regulates menstrual flow, used for uterine complaints, diabetes, rheumatic pain |
| **Akar Saga** | *Abrus precatorius* | Seeds, leaves, roots | Diuretic and laxative (seeds are poisonous); roots used for gonorrhoea and jaundice |

## 4.3 Landscape Plants

**Landscape plants** beautify gardens in and around house compounds, office buildings, public playing grounds, city streets, golf courses, and recreation areas. They fall into **four groups** — **groundcovers, shrubs, palms, and trees** — which are combined for their texture and colour.

### Shrubs
A **shrub** is a small-to-medium perennial woody plant with persistent woody stems, distinguished from trees by having **multiple stems** and a shorter height (under 6–10 m). Shrubs may be **deciduous** or **evergreen**; a dense, much-branched shrub may be called a bush.
- Examples: *Baphia nitida*, *Calathea lutea*, *Cananga fruticosa*, *Ervatamia coronaria*, *Excoecaria cochinchinensis*, Hydrangea.

![Row of evergreen ornamental shrubs in a landscaped garden](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Row_of_evergreen_ornamental_shrubs_in_a_landscaped_garden%2C_Onitsha%2C_Nigeria.jpg?width=600)
*Image: "Row of evergreen ornamental shrubs in a landscaped garden, Onitsha, Nigeria" by Confidence24, Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0).*

### Groundcover
Any low plant that grows over an area of ground, protecting topsoil from **erosion and drought**. In an ecosystem it forms the **herbaceous layer** below the shrub layer (also called "ground flora" or "regenerative layer"); the most widespread groundcovers are grasses.
- Examples: *Arachis pintoi*, *Portulaca grandiflora*, *Galium odoratum*, Dwarf periwinkle (*Vinca minor*), *Thymus serpyllum*, *Cerastium tomentosum*.

### Trees
A **tree** is a perennial plant with an elongated trunk that usually supports branches and leaves (in a wider sense, tall palms, tree ferns, bananas, and bamboos also count). Trees are **not a taxonomic group** — most species are **angiosperms/hardwoods**, with the remainder **gymnosperms/softwoods**; many live for centuries.

| Leaf habit | Meaning | Examples |
| ---------- | ------- | -------- |
| **Deciduous** | Sheds leaves seasonally, then blooms vigorously again | Tecoma tree (*Tabebuia rosea*), Flame of the forest (*Butea monosperma*), Yellow flame (*Peltophorum pterocarpum*), Weeping willow (*Salix babylonica*), Red maple (*Acer rubrum*) |
| **Evergreen** | Maintains its foliage year-round | Pokok Kelat Paya (*Cinnamomum iners*), Rain tree (*Samanea saman*), Tanjong tree (*Mimusops elengi*) |

### Palms
One of the best-known and most cultivated plant families, important to humans throughout history (many common products and foods derive from palms) and widely used in landscaping today. Most palms are distinguished by large, compound, evergreen leaves (**fronds**) arranged at the top of an unbranched stem, and the family shows huge diversity — from rainforest to desert habitats.
- Examples: Fan palm (*Hyphaene petersiana*), Foxtail palm (*Wodyetia bifurcata*), Royal palm (*Roystonea regia*), Date palm (*Phoenix dactylifera*), Coconut (*Cocos nucifera*), Acai palm (*Euterpe oleracea*).

## 4.4 Turf Grass

**Turf** is the horticultural term for grass that is mowed and maintained in the same way as a lawn. It is valuable for both **aesthetic appeal** and **environmental contributions**:
- Protects soil from erosion.
- Captures runoff water.
- Reduces dust and heat irradiation.
- Acts as a natural cushion for recreational sport and relaxation.

**Bermuda grass** is the standard golf-course grass — it has high heat tolerance, can be mowed very low without damage, withstands dry spells, and needs relatively little maintenance (e.g. used at the Kuala Lumpur Golf & Country Club, Bukit Kiara).

## Summary

- **Nursery industry:** a nursery raises plants for eventual planting out through **propagation, media preparation, and potting/re-potting**; six types exist (fruit, vegetable, medicinal/aromatic, ornamental, forest, hi-tech), supported by facilities like shade houses, mist units, and fertiliser/chemical stores.
- **Propagation methods:** **stem cutting** (softwood, semi-hardwood, hardwood), **bud grafting** (wedge cut), and **marcotting/air layering** (girdle → moist moss → wrap → cut rooted marcot).
- **Herbs and medicinal plants:** valued for medicinal compounds (e.g. antioxidants); different plant parts (roots, flower buds, fruits, leaf, bark/resins) are used depending on the species, forming a global business forecast at RM60 billion/year.
- **Landscape plants:** four groups — **shrubs** (woody, multi-stemmed), **groundcover** (protects topsoil), **trees** (deciduous vs evergreen), and **palms** (fronded, unbranched stem) — combined for texture and colour in garden design.
- **Turf grass:** mowed lawn grass (e.g. Bermuda grass) valued for both looks and erosion/runoff control, widely used on golf courses and recreational fields.
