# Topic 5: Livestock Industry

**Outline:** 5.1 Livestock & Ruminant/Non-Ruminant Classification · 5.2 Poultry Industry · 5.3 Cattle Farming Systems · 5.4 Cattle Breeds · 5.5 Goats, Sheep & Buffaloes · 5.6 Products, Byproducts & Economic Importance · 5.7 Zoonotic Diseases

> Malaysians eat almost **55 kg of chicken** a year each — but the country produces barely a tenth of the mutton it consumes. Behind every plate of satay or glass of milk sits a livestock industry built on very different animals, farming systems and levels of self-sufficiency. This topic looks at how that industry is organised, from battery cages to oil-palm cattle ranching.

---

## 5.1 Livestock & Ruminant/Non-Ruminant Classification

**Livestock** (FAO definition) — domesticated terrestrial animals raised to provide goods and services such as **traction, meat, milk, eggs, hides, fibres and feathers**.
- Byproducts (**dung, bones, blood**) are recycled as **organic fertiliser** for crop production.

### Two major groups
Livestock are grouped by their digestive system:

| | **Ruminant** | **Non-ruminant** |
| - | ------------- | ----------------- |
| **Diet type** | Herbivore | Omnivore or carnivore |
| **Stomach** | Complex, **four compartments** | Simple, **single compartment** |
| **Digestion** | Chews & regurgitates food (**"chews the cud"**), digested multiple times in different stomach chambers | Food digested once, in one stomach |
| **Sub-groups** | **Big ruminant** (cattle, buffalo) · **Small ruminant** (goat, sheep, deer) | **Poultry** · **Monogastric** (swine, rabbit) |
| **Examples** | Cattle, sheep, goats, buffalo, deer | Rabbit, poultry, pigs |

- Ruminants form the **larger, more important group** in Malaysia's livestock sector.
- Most smallholders own **fewer than 10 heads** of cattle or goats and rear them by **free-grazing** on padi fields (off-season), roadsides, football fields, or fenced government grazing reserves — an unsystematic approach that is **not viable** and risks conflict when animals roam uncontrolled.

### Self-sufficiency and consumption (Malaysia, 2024)
Malaysia is largely self-sufficient in poultry meat and duck meat, but heavily **import-dependent for beef and mutton**:

| Item | Self-sufficiency ratio (%) | Import dependency (%) | Per capita consumption (kg/year) |
| ---- | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| Chicken meat | 92.9 | 11.0 | 54.9 |
| Duck meat | 129.9 | 0.05 | 1.4 |
| Pork | 67.8 | 32.4 | 15.9 |
| Beef/buffalo meat | 16.8 | 83.5 | 6.8 |
| Mutton | 10.6 | 92.1 | 1.5 |

*Source: Supply and Utilization of Selected Agricultural Commodities, Malaysia 2024, Department of Statistics Malaysia.*

## 5.2 Poultry Industry (Non-Ruminant)

**Poultry farming** — raising birds such as chickens, turkeys, ducks and geese (a subcategory of animal husbandry) for **meat or eggs**.

### Three types of chicken farming

| System | Description | Advantages | Disadvantages |
| ------ | ------------ | ---------- | -------------- |
| **1. Free range** | Birds roam outdoors (open/fenced) for at least part of the day; feed/water stations provided but birds may forage on their own | Natural movement & foraging; cleaner living conditions | Little control over diet; unreliable productivity; small-scale only |
| **2. Factory (battery/cage) farming** | Multi-layer cages, high bird density; ventilation, heating & lighting automated; extra light and hormones/growth stimulants boost egg-laying or weight gain. Common for **layers** in Malaysia | Lower feed cost (less movement); economical, high output; less exposure to predators/weather | Reduced stimulation → pecking; ~50% risk of bone disorders (osteoporosis, breakage); higher fire risk; animal-welfare concerns |
| **3. Sheltered enclosure** | Birds kept in large, open "grow-out houses" with litter (rice hulls, wood shavings); mechanical feed/water delivery, ventilation, coolers/heaters | Weather protection; better health/welfare & disease control; easier management, improved productivity | High construction & maintenance cost; disease spreads fast if poorly managed; restricts natural behaviour |

### Poultry breeds
- **Broiler breeds:** Sussex, Orpington, Dorking, Indian Game/Cornish, Brahma, Jersey Giant.
- **Layer breeds:** Australorp, Blue Andalusian, Leghorns, Hamburg, Red Cap, Ancona.
- **Special/ornamental breeds:** Houdan, Bantam, Onagadori, Silkie Bantams, Serama, Sultan.

**Commercial layers in Malaysia:**

| Breed | Eggs/year | Egg colour | Life span | Time to maturity |
| ----- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| Lohmann Brown | up to 320 | Light brown | 6–8 years | 18–20 weeks |
| White Leghorn | 280–320 | White | 4–7 years | 18–20 weeks |

## 5.3 Cattle Farming Systems (Ruminant)

Cattle farming splits into **beef cattle** (*lembu pedaging*) and **dairy cattle** (*lembu tenusu*). Commercial farmers and government departments use **three main systems**.

### 1. Open grazing on improved pasture
- Cattle roam a systematically fertilised, high-nutritive-value pasture.
- Requires **large land area** and constant pasture maintenance.
- Breeds used: **KK–Brahman crosses** (beef) or **Sahiwal–Friesian crosses** (dairy).
- Each animal eats ~**3% of body weight** (dry weight) daily.
- **Low stocking rate**: only ~5–6 heads/ha — a key reason few commercial ranches in Malaysia use this system (high land cost, low profitability).
- **Pasture grasses:** buffalo/cow grass, guinea grass, pangola grass, ruzi/congo grass, signal grass, elephant grass.
- **Pasture legumes** (enrich soil + feed): centro, calopo, puero, pinto peanut.

### 2. Feedlot system
- Animals kept in **sheltered houses** and fed high-quality feed to fatten them quickly — about **6 months**, until reaching **320–350 kg**.
- Much higher stocking density: ~**250 heads/ha**.
- Feed cost is the main constraint — **60–70% of total production cost**; a 200 kg animal needs ~6 kg dry feed/day, rising as it grows (e.g. ~RM1,200 for 6 months on 100% pellet feed).
- Feed = commercial pellets + freshly cut pasture + silage + mineral salt, often supplemented with **agricultural byproducts**: palm kernel cake (PKC), rice bran, cocoa pods, soybean waste, sago waste, fruit peels (e.g. jackfruit).
- Suitable breeds: imported (**Brahman, Droughtmaster, Nellore**), crossbred (**Brakmas**, KK × Nellore), local (**KK**).

**Silage** — feed made from fermented pasture or agricultural biomass (maize, sorghum, etc.). Benefits: can be **stored long-term**, increases byproduct utilisation, cuts feed cost, and reduces pollution from open burning/waste dumping.

### 3. Oil palm integration system
- Cattle graze on **understory vegetation** (shrubs, grasses, legumes) within oil palm/rubber plantations, reducing the farmer's weeding cost.
- Introduced only once palms reach a suitable age (to avoid damaging young stands < 18 months).
- **Mob grazing** for initial weed control: 400–500 heads on a 40 ha lot, moved daily with electric fencing, until weeds are suppressed.
- **Continuous commercial grazing** stocking rate: **1 animal/ha** (good understory) or **1 animal/2 ha** (poor understory).

| | Advantages | Disadvantages |
| - | ---------- | -------------- |
| **Oil palm–cattle integration** | Lower weeding & fertiliser cost (cattle eat weeds, dung fertilises soil); less herbicide use & pollution; optimises land/resource use, extra income stream | Soil compaction, drainage & plant damage from uncontrolled grazing; injury to young palms; extra labour/fencing costs |

## 5.4 Cattle Breeds

### Bos taurus vs Bos indicus

| | **Bos taurus** | **Bos indicus** |
| - | -------------- | ---------------- |
| **Origin** | Europe | India/Asia |
| **Climate** | Temperate | Tropical/subtropical, humid |
| **Parasite resistance** | Low | High (e.g. ticks) |
| **Hump** | Absent | Present (thoracic/cervicothoracic) |
| **Head** | Short & wide | Long |
| **Milk production** | Higher | Lower |
| **Carcass trait** | Positive | Decreased |

- **Beef cattle breeds:** *Bos taurus* — Angus, Charolais, Chianina, Gelbvieh, Hereford, Limousin; *Bos indicus* — Brahman, Kedah-Kelantan (KK), Nelore; **crossbreds** — Brangus, Brakmas, Draughtmaster.
- **Local breed:** **Kedah-Kelantan (KK)** — small, adaptable to local conditions, tick-resistant.
- **Brakmas** (50% Brahman × 50% KK) — better growth than KK; birth weight 20.2 kg, weaning weight 97 kg, mature weight 280 kg; resistant to tropical environment and ecto-parasites; used commercially in oil palm integration.
- **Dairy cattle:** local — **Local Indian Dairy (LID)**, **Sahiwal-Friesian crosses**; temperate — Jersey, Friesian, Australian Milking Zebu (AMZ).

### Spotlight: Wagyu cattle
- *Wagyu* = "Japanese cattle." Non-crossed Japanese breeds: **Japanese Black (90%)**, Japanese Brown (9%), Japanese Shorthorn (<1%), Japanese Polled (rare) — collectively called "Wagyu" in the US.
- **Feeding:** Japanese Black cattle are fed a **high-concentrate diet from 11–30 months** of age (fed 2–3×/day) to build intramuscular fat, consuming 4,000–5,000 kg of feed/head in total; **90% of the concentrate is imported**.
- **Result:** highly **marbled** meat with softer fat and a higher proportion of monounsaturated fatty acids compared to normal beef.

## 5.5 Goats, Sheep & Buffaloes

### Goat farming
- Malaysia imports **87.9%** of its mutton (2019 data) — commercial-scale goat/sheep farming is uncommon; most are smallholder-reared, often without a systematic approach.
- A **shed** (with running yard) is essential — goats are adapted to hot climates and get sick if exposed to rain.
- System resembles cattle **feedlot**: animals fed commercial pellets and freshly cut fodder daily, using similar pasture species to cattle.

**Popular breeds:**
- **Meat goat:** Boer, Katjang, Savannah, Black Bengal.
- **Dairy goat:** Saanen, Alpine, Toggenburg.
- **Dual purpose (dairy + meat):** Jamnapari.
- **Local breed — Katjang** (*Capra aegagrus*) — the only indigenous Malaysian goat breed; naturally tolerant of heat and disease in the hot, humid equatorial climate.
- Other breeds shown: British Alpine, Anglo-Nubian, Toggenburg.
- **Sheep breeds:** Dorper (meat), and **Malin** — Malaysia's local sheep breed.

![A Boer goat, a popular meat-goat breed](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Boer_goat444.jpg?width=600)
*Image: "Boer goat" by Benjamint444, Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 3.0).*

### Buffaloes
- Malaysia's buffalo population is small — only around **120,000** head.
- **Murrah buffalo** — reared for **milk**.
- **Water buffalo** — reared for **meat**; also used for **transporting oil palm fruit bunches**.
- In Sabah and other states, cattle, buffaloes and ponies are decorated and used in **cultural shows and races** during Harvest Festivals.

## 5.6 Products, Byproducts & Economic Importance

| | Products | Byproducts |
| - | -------- | ---------- |
| **Poultry** | Meat, eggs | Fertiliser (manure), biodiesel, animal feed, biogas, bone powder, biodegradable plastics, gelatin (from feet) |
| **Ruminant** | Milk, meat | Blood, bones, tendons, meat trimmings, fat/tallow, hides, hooves, horns, internal organs, viscera, feet → used for fertiliser, fuel & pharmaceuticals, animal feed, pet food |

### Why livestock matters economically
- **Source of income:** ~**68%** of households earn income from livestock (Davis et al., 2007).
- **Economic contributor:** domestic and international trade of livestock products.
- **Employment:** jobs across the value chain — production, trading, processing, retail.
- **Asset to farmers:** provides quick cash and serves as a valuable, liquid asset.
- **Manure:** recycled to fertilise crop fields.

## 5.7 Zoonotic Diseases

**Zoonotic diseases (zoonoses)** — infections shared between animals and humans, caused by viruses, bacteria, fungi or parasites.
- **Direct zoonosis** — transmitted straight from animal to human, e.g. through air (influenza) or bites/saliva (rabies).

**Examples spread by livestock:**
- **Bird flu / Avian influenza (H5N1)** — natural hosts include many waterfowl species.
- **Bovine Spongiform Encephalopathy (BSE) / "Mad Cow Disease."**
- **Japanese Encephalitis (JE)** — caused by a flavivirus; transmitted by infected mosquitoes; **pigs and wading birds** are the main carriers.
- **E. coli** (*Escherichia coli*).
- **Salmonellosis** (*Salmonella* spp.).

## Summary

- **Livestock** provide meat, milk, eggs, hides, fibres and traction; byproducts recycle as fertiliser.
- **Ruminants** (cattle, buffalo, goat, sheep — 4-chamber stomach) vs **non-ruminants** (poultry, swine, rabbit — single stomach). Malaysia is largely self-sufficient in chicken/duck but imports most beef and mutton.
- **Poultry:** three systems — free range, factory/battery cage, sheltered enclosure — each trading off cost, welfare and productivity; broiler, layer and special breeds differ by purpose.
- **Cattle:** beef vs dairy; three systems — open pasture grazing (low stocking, high land use), feedlot (fast fattening, high feed cost), oil palm integration (cost-saving but needs careful management). *Bos taurus* (temperate) vs *Bos indicus* (tropical, tick-resistant); local KK and Brakmas breeds; Wagyu prized for marbling.
- **Goats/sheep:** mostly smallholder, shed-based, feedlot-style rearing; Katjang and Malin are Malaysia's local breeds.
- **Buffaloes:** small population (~120,000); Murrah for milk, water buffalo for meat and draught work.
- **Economic role:** major income source (68% of households), driver of trade, employment and farm assets.
- **Zoonotic risks:** avian influenza, BSE, Japanese encephalitis, E. coli and Salmonella can pass from livestock to humans.
