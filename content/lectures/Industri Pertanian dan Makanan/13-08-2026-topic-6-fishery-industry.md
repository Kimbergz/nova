# Topic 6: Fishery Industry

**Outline:** 6.1 Fisheries Industry Subsector · 6.2 Aquaculture · 6.3 Seaweed Farming · 6.4 Non-Food Fish Sector (Ornamental Fish) · 6.5 Site Selection for Aquaculture · 6.6 Fishing Methods and Technology

> In 2021, Malaysia's fisheries industry produced **1.75 million metric tonnes** of edible fish, **242.5 million pieces** of ornamental fish, and **24.4 million bunches** of aquatic plants — together worth **RM14.88 billion**. The sector exists to ensure an adequate food supply and to guarantee people's access to fish as a source of dietary protein.

---

## 6.1 Fisheries Industry Subsector

The fisheries industry is made up of **marine capture fisheries** and **inland fisheries**.

### Marine capture fisheries
Split into two main types based on distance from shore:

| | Coastal / inshore fisheries | Deep sea fisheries |
| - | ---------------------------- | ------------------- |
| **Range** | Within **30 nautical miles** of the coastline | Beyond **30 nautical miles** from the shoreline |
| **Target species** | **Pelagic species** (live near the surface) | **Demersal/benthic species** (live near the seabed) |
| **Status** | Already at **maximum level of exploitation** | Fishers moving further offshore as technology improves |
| **Examples** | Shrimp, Indian mackerel, round scads, squid, tuna, anchovies, ox-eye scads | — |

*Image: "Kapal Nelayan Purse Seine 1" by Toriqun Nuril Anwar, Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0).*

### Inland fisheries
- **Inland waters** = lakes, rivers, brooks, streams, ponds, inland canals, dams, and other land-locked waters.
- Play an important role in **sustainably feeding a growing population** in an accessible, affordable way.
- Provide an important source of **nutrition, food security, and micronutrients**.

### Invasive species
**Invasive species** = an organism not indigenous/native to an area, introduced by human activity (deliberately or accidentally) to areas outside its native range, causing ecological or economic harm.

**Impacts:**
- Introduces and spreads new disease outbreaks.
- Disrupts the biodiversity balance of native fish habitats.
- Dominates and reproduces in natural habitats, becoming a **pest or predator** of indigenous fish.
- Causes **great economic losses** to farmers and the aquaculture industry.

## 6.2 Aquaculture

**Aquaculture (aquafarming)** = the breeding and cultivation of aquatic organisms (fish, shrimp, mollusks, aquatic plants) for commercial purposes. It is a crucial part of the global food industry, supplying protein and food.

### Main purposes of aquaculture in Malaysia
1. **Increase fish production for food security** — meet domestic demand for fish/seafood and reduce dependence on capture fisheries.
2. **Support economic growth and export earnings** — contributes significantly to agriculture GDP; shrimp, grouper, tilapia and catfish have export potential.
3. **Restore threatened/endangered species populations** — via **broodstock banks** and breeding in controlled environments.
4. **Wild stock population enhancement** — juveniles/larvae raised in hatcheries, then released into natural ecosystems.
5. **Habitat restoration** — linked projects such as **mangrove replanting** and **artificial reefs**.

### Types of aquaculture (by water type)
- **Mariculture** — farming aquatic plants/animals in **salt water**.
- **Freshwater aquaculture** — culture within a **freshwater** environment.
- **Brackish water** — a mix of fresh and salty water, typically found in **estuaries**.

### Aquaculture systems

| System | Key idea | Examples | Advantages | Disadvantages |
| ------ | -------- | -------- | ---------- | -------------- |
| **1. Open system** | Oldest system; uses the natural sea/lake as the farm; no water pumping needed | Clam trays, oyster rafts, fish cages | Low capital (land can be leased); less management; natural, uncrowded environment | Predation & poaching; variable growth rate and yield uniformity |
| **2. Semi-closed system** | Most popular method; produces organisms at densities higher than nature; water replaced regularly; pumping, aeration, commercial feeds used | Fish in ponds, cages, net pens; **raceways** (continuous water flow, circular/rectangular) | Greater control (temperature, water volume, current); less feed waste; less poaching; higher/uniform production; easier disease treatment | Expensive to build/operate; complex management; higher disease/stress risk |
| **3. Closed system** | Land-based rearing in raceways, tanks, ponds; little/no water exchange; can be very productive | **Recirculating system** — filters and cycles water back into the system (used at the UPM fish breeding facility) | Full control of growing conditions; no environmental parasites/predators/disease; weather is not a problem; easy, uniform harvest | High capital, maintenance & operating cost (machinery-dependent); undetected disease can destroy large yields |
| **4. Integrated system** | Combines fish culture with crop and animal production | — | Less additional expense; minimal adverse effect on other farming; diversifies farm outputs; follows a "no waste, only misplaced resources" concept | — |

## 6.3 Seaweed Farming

- **Sabah** and **Sarawak** (East Malaysia) have great potential for seaweed culture; species grow naturally around **Semporna, Banggi Island,** and **Kudat** (Sabah).
- **Main groups cultivated for human food:** red algae, brown algae, green algae.
- **Products made from seaweed:** food, drugs, cosmetic products.

**Methods of culturing seaweed:**
1. **Bottom culture** — in shallow, sandy areas; posts placed in the sand opposite each other, rope tied post to post, seedlings tied onto the rope.
2. **Semi-raft culture** — used only in lagoons, at 10–20 ft depth; pegged stakes on opposite sides of the lagoon with floats tied to the monoline about 3 ft below the surface.

In Sabah, seaweed farming is promoted among fishermen on islands or near the coast as an **alternative income**, especially during the monsoon season, and to **discourage "fish bombing."**

## 6.4 Non-Food Fish Sector: Ornamental Fish

Fish kept in home aquariums or for aesthetic purposes. The industry is supported by four categories of fish keepers:

| Category | Description | Examples |
| -------- | ----------- | -------- |
| **1. Beginners** | Largest market; hardy, need no special care, easy to breed, low production cost | Angelfish, guppies, gourami, some barbs |
| **2. Collectors** | Specialists who bring together different species/strains of the same genus; need special care; small niche market | Betta, Rasbora |
| **3. Biotope enthusiasts** | Like the challenge of creating a self-sustaining ecosystem, decorated with varied aquatic flora and fauna | — |
| **4. Feng shui practitioners** | Buy/keep fish for their perceived value in bringing good luck | Arowana, Koi, Blood-parrot Cichlid, Flowerhorn |

## 6.5 Site Selection for Aquaculture

Six factors determine a good aquaculture site: **cost, water quality, topography & location, climate condition, access to infrastructure, and soil quality.**

### 1. Cost
- **Land** — rent or self-owned.
- **Construction & consultation** — pond design, type, and size.
- **Labour** (salary, wage) — amount required for efficient construction, smooth operation, and sustainable profitability.

### 2. Water quality
**Physical:**
- **Temperature** must match the fish species.
- **Colour** — ideal is light green; brown/red/dark/smelly water signals poor quality.
- **Turbidity** — high turbidity (too cloudy) means low oxygen; low turbidity (too clear) means little natural food (plankton) for fish.

**Biological** — water should be free from:
- **Predators** — predator fish (snakehead, catfish), aquatic insects (backswimmer, giant water bug) that reduce fry populations; also snakes, otters, kingfishers.
- **Pathogens** — e.g. **Tilapia Lake Virus (TiLV)** (high mortality in tilapia), **Ichthyophthirius (Ich)** parasite (white spot disease), **Pseudomonas** bacteria (ulcers, haemorrhages).

**Chemical:**

| Parameter | Concern | Optimal range |
| --------- | ------- | -------------- |
| **Dissolved oxygen** | Essential for respiration; low levels stress/kill fish | > 5 mg/L |
| **pH** | Low pH → stress, low metabolism; high pH → slow growth, gill damage | 6.5–8.5 |
| **Alkalinity** | Right range supports efficient feeding, growth, and disease resistance | 20–150 mg/L |
| **Ammonia** | Waste product from excretion/uneaten feed; high levels lower growth and raise disease risk | < 0.05 mg/L |
| **Nitrate** | High levels cause eutrophication and algal bloom, reducing dissolved oxygen | < 50 mg/L |
| **Salinity** | Too high → osmotic imbalance, gill damage; too low → poor reproduction, weak immunity | 0–5 ppt (freshwater fish) |

### 3. Topography & location
- Farm location/site must be **approved by relevant authorities**.
- Needs **sufficient, continuous water resources**.
- Pollution/contamination risk must be controllable.
- **Avoid:** lowland areas (flooding), hillsides (soil erosion), and areas near residential zones.

### 4. Climate
- Must suit the selected fish species, or an **indoor culture system** is needed.
- **Salmon** (temperate fish) needs 20–25 °C — **not suitable for Malaysia**.
- **Tilapia** needs higher temperatures (above 25 °C) — not suited to temperate countries.

### 5. Access to infrastructure
- **Good electricity supply** — powers aerators, pumps, filtration, lighting, heaters, automatic feeders.
- **Processing facilities** — reduce post-harvest loss.
- **Proper roads** — faster, safer delivery to market/processors.
- **Technical assistance** — improves survival, growth, and farm performance.
- **Drainage system** — separate inlet/outlet drains prevent disease spread and ease water management.

### 6. Soil quality
- **Texture:** clay = small particles (should be **≥ 45%** for good water retention); sand = large particles (should be **< 27%**); sandy clay and clay+loam mixes work well.
- **Soil pH:** best range is **6–9**. If too acidic, add **lime** to raise pH; if too high, add **potassium permanganate** to lower it.

## 6.6 Fishing Methods and Technology

Fish-catching methods fall into two broad categories: **traditional methods** and **current methods**, alongside **modern technology** used to support fisheries.

### 1. Traditional methods
Practiced for generations — simple, low-cost, and suitable for small-scale fisheries.

| Gear | How it works |
| ---- | ------------- |
| **Bubu** | Trap made from bamboo or rattan; fish enter through an opening and are retained inside |
| **Spear** | Fish caught one by one using a spear/harpoon; common in shallow water at night |
| **Casting net** | Circular net thrown by hand; sinks quickly to trap fish in shallow water |
| **Serkap** | Fixed bamboo fish trap placed in rivers/estuaries; fish enter with the tidal current |
| **Serkap (beach seine)** | Large seine net pulled ashore by hand; effective in shallow coastal areas |
| **Fence / kelong** | Fish guided by fences or corral systems of stakes and nets in tidal areas |

**Key features of traditional methods:** environmentally friendly (low ecosystem impact), low cost (simple tools/local materials), community-based (supports local livelihoods), and sustainable when used responsibly and seasonally.

### 2. Current methods
Modern fishing vessels and gear used to improve efficiency, safety, and catch quality while supporting sustainable fisheries:
- **Purse seiner** — large surrounding net drawn closed at the bottom like a purse to catch fish in open waters (operates in designated zones, e.g. Zone B, C, C2).
- **Support vessel** — provides logistics, fuel, ice, food, and other supplies to fishing operations at sea.
- **Trawler** — tows a net along the seabed or mid-water to catch fish in coastal or offshore areas, using mechanized trawling gear to catch a wide variety of species.

Vessels are also classified by **Gross Registered Tonnage (GRT)** into fishing zones — from the **Conservation Zone** (0–1 nm, community/aquaculture use only) through **Zones A, B, B1, C & C2** (1–15 nm and beyond, increasing GRT limits) to **Zone C3** (70 GRT and above, out to the Indian Ocean / Exclusive Economic Zone).

### 3. Modern technology
Trawlers are increasingly equipped with high-technology systems:

**A. Potential Fishing Site Identification System (FSI)**
- Three main components: **satellite image processing**, a **database**, and an **information dissemination system**.
- Highly potential fishing sites are identified as coordinates and sent to registered fishermen via SMS (**MySMS-15888**).
- **Advantages:** locates potential fishing grounds quickly; reduces fuel costs; increases total catch.

**B. Vessel Monitoring System (VMS)**
- A **satellite-based system** that reports a vessel's position and activity.
- Key components: **Vessel Tracking Unit** (sensor) and **Mobile Tracking Unit (MTU)**, which obtains vessel position via **GPS**.
- **Benefits:** supports fleet management; acts as a communication tool; helps the department protect fishery resources.

## Summary

- **Fisheries subsectors:** marine capture (coastal/inshore for pelagic species vs. deep sea for demersal species) and inland fisheries (lakes, rivers, ponds — key for food security and nutrition); watch for **invasive species** harming native stocks.
- **Aquaculture** breeds/cultivates aquatic organisms for food security, export earnings, endangered-species restoration, wild-stock enhancement, and habitat restoration; can be **mariculture, freshwater,** or **brackish water**.
- **Four aquaculture systems:** open (natural, low-cost, less controlled) → semi-closed (ponds/cages/raceways, most popular) → closed (land-based, recirculating, high control) → integrated (combined with crop/animal farming).
- **Seaweed farming** thrives in Sabah/Sarawak using bottom culture or semi-raft culture, providing food, drug, and cosmetic products plus alternative fisherman income.
- **Ornamental fish sector** serves beginners, collectors, biotope enthusiasts, and feng shui practitioners.
- **Site selection** for aquaculture weighs cost, water quality (physical/biological/chemical), topography, climate, infrastructure access, and soil quality.
- **Fishing methods** range from traditional gear (bubu, spear, casting net, serkap, kelong) to current vessels (purse seiners, trawlers, support vessels, zoned by GRT) supported by modern technology (FSI, VMS).
