# Topic 7: Introduction to Forestry and Agro-Forestry

**Outline:** 7.1 What Is a Forest? · 7.2 Forestry in Malaysia · 7.3 Importance of Forests · 7.4 Forest Tree Species & Forestry Products · 7.5 Agroforestry · 7.6 Forestry Issues & Conservation

> Forests cover more than half of Peninsular Malaysia and quietly do the work of **filtering our water, storing carbon, and holding hillsides together**. This topic looks at how Malaysia classifies and governs its forests, what they give us, and how **agroforestry** — deliberately growing trees together with crops and livestock — lets us keep some of those benefits even on farmland.

---

## 7.1 What Is a Forest?

A **forest** is a large area of land covered with trees and plants, usually larger than a wood, or the trees and plants themselves *(Cambridge Dictionary)*.

Forests are classified by how much they have been disturbed by people:

| | Primary forest | Secondary forest |
| - | --------------- | ----------------- |
| **Definition** | An untouched, "virgin" forest | A forest already logged or significantly disturbed by human activity |
| **Trees** | Lots of large-trunk trees; large, spreading crowns | Smaller trees; smallish, cone/triangular-shaped crowns |
| **Canopy** | Visible **emergent layer**; sparse undergrowth | Poorly defined emergent layer; low canopy of immature trees |

### Layers of a (tropical) forest
From the ground up, a forest is structured into four layers:
- **Emergent layer** — the tallest trees, poking above the main canopy (~125 ft / 38 m).
- **Canopy layer** — the dense, continuous roof of tree crowns (~95 ft / 29 m).
- **Understory layer** — smaller trees and shrubs beneath the canopy (~55 ft / 17 m).
- **Forest floor** — the ground layer of leaf litter, seedlings and undergrowth (~15 ft / 5 m).

## 7.2 Forestry in Malaysia

### Forest statistics (Peninsular Malaysia, 2023)
| Type of area | Million hectares |
| ------------- | ----------------- |
| Total area of Peninsular Malaysia | 13.18 |
| — Forested area | 5.74 |
| — Non-forested area | 7.44 |
| Permanent Reserved Forest (Peninsular) | 4.85 |
| Permanent Reserved Forest (Sabah) | 3.57 |
| Permanent Reserved Forest (Sarawak) | 3.69 |

Within Peninsular Malaysia's Permanent Reserved Forest (4.85 million ha): **inland forest** (4.41M ha) dominates, with smaller areas of **peat swamp forest** (0.21M ha), **forest plantation** (0.14M ha) and **mangroves** (0.09M ha).

### Forest types by altitude
Malaysia's forest type changes with elevation and coastal proximity:

| Forest type | Elevation | Key species / features |
| ----------- | --------- | ----------------------- |
| **Mangrove** | Coastal mudflats, lagoons, estuaries | Tolerates salt water and tides; **stilt roots** and **pneumatophores** (e.g. *Rhizophora* spp.) |
| **Peat swamp** | ~6 m, aqueous & acidic peat soil behind the coastline | Kempas, Bintangor, Meranti paya, Ramin, Nyatoh, Resak |
| **Lowland Dipterocarp** | Below 300 m | Widest variety of species; Meranti, Balau, Kapur |
| **Hill Dipterocarp** | 300–750 m | Most extensive forest type; Meranti seraya, Keruing |
| **Upper Dipterocarp** | 750–1,200 m | Meranti bukit, Damar minyak, Bintangor, Keruing |
| **Montane** | 1,200–1,500 m | Berangan, Medang, Keruing, Resak, Mempening |
| **Ericaceous** | Above 1,500 m | Small trees; bamboo, fern, moss |

### Governance
Under **Article 74(2) of the Federal Constitution**, forestry is a **state matter** — each state enacts its own laws and manages its own forestry policy. The federal government's role is limited to advice, technical assistance, training, and R&D.

| Region | Governing body | Key legislation |
| ------ | --------------- | ---------------- |
| **Peninsular Malaysia** | Forestry Department of Peninsular Malaysia (FDPM) + State Forestry Departments | National Forestry Act 1984 (amended 1993); Forestry Policy of Peninsular Malaysia |
| **Sabah** | Sabah Forestry Department (SFD) | Forest Enactment 1968; Forest Rules 1969; Forest (Timber) Enactment 2015; Sabah Forest Policy |
| **Sarawak** | Forest Department Sarawak (FDS) | Forests Ordinance 2015 (Cap. 71); Forests Regulations; Sarawak Forest Policy |

### Permanent Reserved Forest (Hutan Simpanan Kekal, HSK)
Land gazetted for forestry purposes under the National Forestry Act 1984; administered by the **State Forestry Department**, with forest products belonging to the state government. HSK is split into:
- **Production forest** — timber harvesting, and quarrying/mining permitted under permit.
- **Protection forest** — fully protected from destructive human activity.

Under Section 10 of the Act, HSK can be gazetted into **11 functional classes**, including: Timber Production Forest, Soil Protection Forest, Soil Reclamation Forest, Flood Control Forest, Water Catchment Forest, Forest Sanctuary for Wildlife, Virgin Jungle Reserve, Amenity Forest, Education Forest, Research Forest, and Forest for Federal Purposes.

**National Forest Policy** aims to: maximise social/economic/environmental benefits sustainably; run planned conservation and silviculture programmes; promote forestry education and public awareness; protect biodiversity and unique flora/fauna; reserve land for education and research; and establish plantations of local and introduced species to reduce pressure on natural forests.

**Malaysia Policy on Forestry (2021)** rests on five pillars: **sustainable forest management**, **protection & conservation of biodiversity**, **climate change mitigation & adaptation**, **application of latest forest technology**, **strengthening R&D**, and **enhancing knowledge sharing**.

## 7.3 Importance of Forests

1. **Act as carbon sinks** — forests absorb more carbon (via photosynthesis) than they release, storing it in biomass, dead organic matter, and soil. **Primary forest** is the most biodiverse and carbon-dense forest type.
2. **Water catchment areas** — highland forests catch rainwater that feeds rivers; about **90% of Malaysia's water needs** come from rivers sourced in highland forests, supplying Kelantan, Perak, Pahang, Selangor and Negeri Sembilan with clean water free of silt.
3. **Prevention of landslides** — highland forests slow the downhill flow of rainwater and help retain it; forest loss removes topsoil, blocks rivers, and increases downstream sedimentation.
4. **Habitat for flora** — Malaysia hosts at least **15,500 species of vascular plants**; its forests are dominated by the **Dipterocarpaceae** family, named for their winged, two-seeded fruit (*di* = two, *ptero* = wing, *carp* = seed). Examples: teak, mahogany, sentang, kapur, rasak, cengal.
5. **Habitat for fauna** — Malaysia has about **1,671 known species** of amphibians, birds, mammals and reptiles; forest clearing directly causes habitat loss and species endangerment.

## 7.4 Forest Tree Species and Forestry Products

### 1. Timber
Wood prepared for building and carpentry. Trees are managed for **sustainable development**, typically taking **30–40 years** before the trunk is thick enough to be felled. Malaysian timber species are grouped into four categories by **wood hardness**:

| Category | Density (at 15% MC) | Characteristics | Example species |
| -------- | -------------------- | ---------------- | ----------------- |
| **Heavy hardwood** | 800–1,120 kg/m³ | High natural durability (toxic tissue compounds like alkaloids repel pests); safe to use unpreserved | Balau, Chengal, Merbau, Red Balau, Mahogany, Teak |
| **Medium hardwood** | 720–880 kg/m³ | Moderate durability; good for medium–heavy construction | Kasai, Kelat, Keledang, Kempas, Keruing, Mengkulang |
| **Light hardwood** | 400–720 kg/m³ | Low durability in the tropics; needs preservation; used for furniture & panels | Bintangor, Dark Red Meranti, Gerutu, Mersawa, Nyatoh, Rubberwood |
| **Softwood** | 385–735 kg/m³ | No natural tropical durability (has tracheids, not vessels/pores); good for panels & plywood | Damar minyak, Podo, Sempilur |

**Malaysia's forest production** includes (in million m³): logs 3.91, sawn timber 2.67, plywood 0.37, mouldings 0.19, veneer 0.09.

A notable example is **Chengal** (*Neobalanocarpus heimii*) — the oldest and largest recorded Chengal, found in Hutan Simpan Pasir Raja, Terengganu, stands **65 m tall with a 16.75 m girth**, the largest tree in Peninsular Malaysia.

### 2. Non-timber products
- **Food** — honey, sago, kabung (sugar palm), fruits.
- **Medicinal plants** — Tongkat ali (*Eurycoma longifolia*), Senduduk (*Melastoma malabathricum*), Pokok kapal terbang (*Eupatorium odoratum*).

## 7.5 Agroforestry

**Agroforestry** is the intentional integration of trees and shrubs into crop and animal farming systems to create environmental, economic, and social benefits *(US Department of Agriculture)*.

**It comprises:**
- Trees on farms and in agricultural landscapes.
- Farming in forests and along forest margins.
- Tree-crop production — including **cocoa, coffee, rubber and oil palm**.

**Agroforestry helps balance:**
- Producing trees for **timber** and other commercial purposes.
- Producing a diverse, adequate supply of **nutritious food** to meet global demand and growers' own needs.
- Protecting the **natural environment**.

![Traditional shade-grown coffee plantation under a canopy of trees](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Traditional%20shade%20coffee%20plantation%20Chikmagalur%202.jpg?width=600)
*Image: "Traditional shade coffee plantation, Chikmagalur" by Anand Osuri, Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0).*

### Case study: Sabah Forest Industry / Sabah Softwoods Berhad (SSB)
- SSB was the **pioneer company** in large-scale commercial tree plantation in Malaysia (timber plantation in Sipitang, Sabah).
- It originally intended to grow **softwoods** (mainly *Pinus caribaea*) for pulp and timber.
- Due to slow growth and difficulty sourcing seed, softwoods were replaced with **three fast-growing hardwoods**: *Acacia mangium* (AM), *Gmelina arborea* (GA), *Albizia falcataria* (AF), and later *Eucalyptus pellita* (EP).

### Impact of agroforestry
- **Reduces deforestation** and helps counter **global warming and hunger**.
- **Clearer water** and improved **air and water quality** — cleaner air/water support healthy growth and development.
- **Biodiversity conservation** — protects and preserves species, habitats, ecosystems and genetic diversity.
- **Stabilises depleted soil from erosion** — erosion is the loss of fertile topsoil to water, wind and tillage.
- **Reduces rural poverty** by increasing on-farm production, household income, and providing jobs — a **stable income** source.

## 7.6 Forestry Issues and Conservation Efforts

### Issues
- **Deforestation** — more than **12 million hectares** of tropical tree cover were lost in 2020 alone (WRI/University of Maryland analysis), including **4.2 million hectares** of undisturbed primary tropical forest; pandemic lockdowns worsened this by limiting enforcement mobility and forcing urban-to-rural migration.
- **Global warming** — burning now occurs inside rainforests (even wetlands), not just at logged edges. Warmer, drier conditions from global warming and forest loss make forests more prone to fire and pests, whose burning/decay release more carbon — a vicious cycle.
- **Extinction of wildlife** — habitat destruction pushes animals (e.g. tigers, elephants, monkeys) into residential areas.
- **Over-exploitation of forest products** — illegal logging and illegal hunting of exotic animals (e.g. pangolin, python, porcupine).

### Overcoming the issues
- **Conservation** — **PERHILITAN** (Jabatan Perlindungan Hidupan Liar dan Taman Negara) leads wildlife and habitat conservation, running centres such as the National Elephant Conservation Centre (Kuala Gandah, Pahang), Sepilok Orangutan Rehabilitation Centre (Sabah), Bornean Sun Bear Conservation Centre (Sabah), and Sungkai Wildlife Conservation Centre (Perak). **FRIM** (Forest Research Institute Malaysia) leads forestry research and public awareness on forest biodiversity.
- **Preservation** — Permanent Reserved Forests are gazetted and managed by each state's Department of Forestry, covering inland forest, peat swamp, mangrove and plantation forest — **11.672 million hectares** as of 2014.
- **Reforestation** — mangrove replanting is carried out around Peninsular Malaysia (notably Selangor and Kedah) using species such as *Rhizophora mucronata* (Bakau Kurap) and *Rhizophora apiculata* (Bakau Minyak).
- **Forest extension activities** — build interaction between institutions and forest managers through: **education & awareness** (tree planting, recycling campaigns, guided forest trips), **community & livelihood** (eco-tourism, agroforestry/organic-farming workshops, linking experts with communities), and **conservation programmes** (wildlife monitoring, camera traps, schools/villages "adopting" a forest patch).

## Summary

- A **forest** is land dominated by trees, classed as **primary** (untouched) or **secondary** (disturbed), and structured into **emergent, canopy, understory, and forest-floor** layers.
- Malaysia's forests range from coastal **mangroves** and **peat swamps** to **lowland, hill, upper Dipterocarp, montane, and ericaceous** forest with rising altitude; forestry is a **state matter** under Article 74(2), managed through gazetted **Permanent Reserved Forests (HSK)**.
- Forests matter as **carbon sinks**, **water catchments**, **landslide prevention**, and **habitat** for Malaysia's rich flora (15,500+ vascular plant species) and fauna (1,671+ species).
- **Timber** is graded into heavy/medium/light hardwood and softwood by density and durability; Malaysia also yields **non-timber products** like honey, sago, and medicinal plants.
- **Agroforestry** intentionally combines trees with crops/livestock (e.g. shade coffee, cocoa, rubber, oil palm) to supply timber and food while protecting the environment, improving water/air quality, cutting erosion, and raising rural income.
- Key threats — **deforestation, global warming, wildlife extinction, and over-exploitation** — are addressed through **conservation, preservation, reforestation,** and **forest extension** programmes.
