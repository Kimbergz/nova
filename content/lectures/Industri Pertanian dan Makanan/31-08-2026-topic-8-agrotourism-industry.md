# Topic 8: Agrotourism Industry

**Outline:** 8.1 Agrotourism in the Wider Economy · 8.2 Malaysia's Tourism Numbers · 8.3 General Tourism vs Agrotourism · 8.4 The Components of Agrotourism · 8.5 Man-Made Projects for the Agrotourism Industry

> Of the tens of millions of tourists who visit Malaysia each year, roughly **30–40%** take part in some form of agrotourism — yet the industry has only recently been treated as a serious economic strategy. Tourism Malaysia's 2022–2026 Strategic Plan now names agrotourism one of the country's most important niche, high-yield tourism segments. This topic looks at what actually counts as agrotourism, why it matters, and the real farms, parks and resorts built around it.

---

## 8.1 Agrotourism in the Wider Economy

Malaysia's economy is generally split into three broad sectors, and tourism (including agrotourism) is one of the **top revenue earners** in the country:

| Sector | Examples |
|---|---|
| Services | Hospitality, travel, transportation, healthcare, professional services |
| Industry | Electronics, automotive, construction, manufacturing |
| Agriculture | Palm oil, rubber, cocoa, animal husbandry, fisheries |

Key ideas about the **service sector**:

- Service industries are **not** directly concerned with producing physical goods (unlike agriculture and manufacturing).
- They produce value that is primarily **intangible** — customer service, management, advice, knowledge, design, and experiences.
- The service sector is the fastest-growing sector in Malaysia, and it typically requires face-to-face interaction.
- **Tourism** is one of the clearest examples of a service-sector industry.

### What is tourism?

> Tourism is the activity of people travelling to and staying in places outside their usual environment, for leisure, business or other purposes, for **not more than one consecutive year**.

Because tourism sells an experience rather than a physical product, the industry must constantly adapt to changing customer needs — customer satisfaction, safety and enjoyment are its central focus.

### Tourism Malaysia's 2022–2026 Strategic Plan

- **Agrotourism is identified as one of the most important niche, high-yield segments** in the Tourism Malaysia 2022–2026 Strategic Plan.
- The plan involves developing a strategic partnership with **MAFS** (Ministry of Agriculture and Food Security) and **FAMA** (Federal Agricultural Marketing Authority) to create more attractive niche packages and tourism events.

## 8.3 General Tourism vs Agrotourism

The major components of attraction for the **general** tourism industry include:

- Social and pleasure/holiday trips
- Sports and cultural heritage
- Shopping and renewing family ties
- Wildlife and scuba diving

**None of these general attractions are, by definition, agrotourism.** Agrotourism is a distinct niche that the tourism industry has diversified into, alongside ecotourism, medical tourism, geotourism, culinary tourism, and rural tourism.

### Defining agrotourism

> **Agrotourism** focuses tourist activity onto a farm, ranch, or other agricultural setting for the purposes of entertaining and/or educating visitors, while generating income for the farm, ranch or business owner.

The term "agrotourism" is often used interchangeably with **agri-tourism**, **agritourism**, **farm tourism**, and **agricultural tourism**.

**Scale in Malaysia:** of all tourist arrivals in the country, roughly **30–40%** are involved in agrotourism activities. The remaining **60–70%** are drawn to nature and wildlife, social and sports activities, cultural heritage, or relaxing and sun-bathing on islands and beaches — none of which count as agrotourism.

### Why agrotourism matters

- It **generates additional income** for related businesses and opens an avenue for direct marketing to consumers (farmers selling straight to visitors, cutting out the middleman).
- It **enhances the wider tourism industry** by increasing both the volume of visitors to an area and the length of their stay.
- It gives the **local population** the potential to increase their income and creates **new employment opportunities**.
- It helps to **preserve agricultural land**, since land that generates tourism revenue is less likely to be sold off or converted, and it lets states develop new business enterprises around farming.

## 8.4 The Components of Agrotourism

Agrotourism combines the essential elements of **agriculture** and **tourism**, letting visitors experience farm life and rural culture — a model that benefits both farmers and tourists through educational and recreational experiences. It has four broad components:

| Component | What it includes |
|---|---|
| **Farm-based experiences** | Farm tours; animal interaction (feeding, milking, petting); picking fruits, vegetables and flowers |
| **Farm stays & accommodation** | Lodging on farms; experiencing countryside life; farm-based camping sites |
| **Educational experiences for visitors** | Workshops on farming technique; sustainable practices such as composting; cooking with local produce |
| **Agricultural & local product sales** | Farmers' markets selling fresh produce; on-site farm shops; farm-to-table dining |

### Examples from the field

- **MAHA Exhibition** (Malaysian Agriculture, Horticulture and Agrotourism Exhibition) is staged at its permanent site near MARDI headquarters, Serdang. Malaysia also hosts floral festivals at **Cameron Highlands** and **Putrajaya**.
- **C&C Mushroom Cultivation Farm Sdn. Bhd.**, Gerisek, Johor, is a mushroom farm that attracts tourists, traders, students and researchers. Its produce (ganoderma mushroom, shiitake, *tiram* species/oyster mushroom) is sold to hotels and exported to Brunei, Singapore and Hong Kong.
- Recreational **fisheries** also count as agrotourism: squid fishing (*mencandat sotong*) at Pulau Pangkor, and recreational fishing at Royal Belum.

## 8.5 Man-Made Projects for the Agrotourism Industry

Four flagship examples were highlighted in the lecture:

### Balung River Eco Resort, Sabah

- One of Malaysia's most famous agrotourism destinations.
- Crops grown on site include sugar palm, forest species, passion fruit, dragon fruit, and medicinal plants.
- Facilities: hostels, chalets, picnic grounds, meeting rooms, restaurants, swimming pool, karaoke facilities.
- Also runs **processing factories** for noni juice and sugar palm syrup, both of which are exported.

### Taman Botani Negara Shah Alam, Selangor

- A plant-themed leisure park.
- Has an **Animal Park** (feeding animals, horse riding).
- Grows a range of spice, herbaceous and beverage crops — tea, coffee, lime, black pepper, tamarind.
- Other attractions: fishing, an adventure park, camping, a paddy field, and a tropical fruits park.

### Cameron Highlands, Pahang

- The **largest agriculture-based highland resort area** in Malaysia. Its cool climate, farms and natural environment make it one of the country's most famous tourist destinations.
- Well-known sites: Taman Agro Tourism, Boh Tea Plantation, Mount Brinchang, and various bee farms.
- Facilities: hostels, chalets, markets and farms, jungle trekking.

### Taman Warisan Pertanian, Putrajaya

- Conserves a wide range of plant species, including retained **old rubber clones**.
- Other facilities: buggy rides, restaurants, food stalls, *pasar tani* (farmers' market), an exhibition ground, landscaped gardens, and meeting rooms.
- Offers many hands-on activities for tourists, including guided rubber-tapping demonstrations.

## Summary

- Agrotourism directs tourists onto working farms/ranches to entertain and/or educate them while generating income for the owner.
- It is one niche among several (eco-, medical, geo-, culinary and rural tourism) that the wider tourism industry has diversified into — and it is distinct from mainstream attractions like beaches, shopping or cultural heritage.
- About 30–40% of tourist arrivals in Malaysia engage in agrotourism.
- Its four core components are farm-based experiences, farm stays/accommodation, educational experiences, and agricultural/product sales.
- Malaysia's flagship agrotourism sites include Balung River Eco Resort (Sabah), Taman Botani Negara (Shah Alam), Cameron Highlands (Pahang) and Taman Warisan Pertanian (Putrajaya).
