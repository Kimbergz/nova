## 1. Structure of the Periodic Table

Elements are arranged in order of increasing **proton number**. The table has **7 periods** (horizontal rows), **18 groups** (vertical columns) and **4 blocks** (s, p, d, f).

**Periods** — elements in the same period share the same principal quantum number (n); number of electrons increases by 1 across the period.

| Period | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| No. of elements | 2 | 8 | 8 | 18 | 18 | 32 | 32 |
| Highest value of n | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 |

**Groups** — 18 vertical columns; elements in the same group have the same number of valence electrons and valence configuration, so similar chemical properties (the first element of a group is often an exception). Going down a group, the number of shells increases by 1.

Main groups: 1, 2, 13–18. Special names: Group 1 = alkali metals, Group 2 = alkaline earth metals, Group 17 = halogens, Group 18 = noble gases, Groups 3–12 = transition metals.

| Group | Valence e⁻ | Valence config |
|---|---|---|
| 1 (alkali, except H) | 1 | ns¹ |
| 2 (alkaline earth) | 2 | ns² |
| 3–12 (transition) | 3–12 | (n−1)d¹⁻¹⁰ns¹⁻² (Cr: d⁵s¹, Cu: d¹⁰s¹ exceptions) |
| 13–16 | 3–6 | ns²np¹⁻⁴ |
| 17 (halogens) | 7 | ns²np⁵ |
| 18 (noble gases) | 8 | ns²np⁶ |

**Blocks** — classified by which orbital the valence electrons fill:

| Block | Elements | Capacity | Valence config |
|---|---|---|---|
| s | Groups 1–2 | 2 e⁻ | ns¹/ns² |
| p | Groups 13–18 | 6 e⁻ | ns²np¹ → ns²np⁶ |
| d | Transition metals (centre of PT) | 10 e⁻ | (n−1)d¹ns² → (n−1)d¹⁰ns² |
| f | Lanthanides/actinides (2 rows of 15, bottom) | 14 e⁻ | — |

## 2. Effective Nuclear Charge (Zeff) and Shielding

**Zeff** = the net positive charge an electron experiences from the nucleus, i.e. the nuclear charge reduced by shielding/screening from intervening electrons.

**Zeff = Z − S**, where Z = actual nuclear charge (proton number) and S = shielding constant (number of electrons in the inner core shells). The higher the Zeff, the smaller the atomic size, and vice versa.

- **Shielding effect**: inner electrons repel/shield valence electrons from the nucleus's pull. Down a group, more energy levels are added → shielding increases.
- Worked examples: Zeff(F, Z=9, config 1s²2s²2p⁵) = 9 − 2 = +7; Zeff(Na⁺, 1s²2s²2p⁶) = 11 − 2 = +9; Zeff(O²⁻, 1s²2s²2p⁶) = 8 − 2 = +6.

## 3. Atomic Radius

Distance between two touching nuclei, halved: **atomic radius = ½d**. Measured in pm or Å.

**Across a period (e.g. Li→F, Na→Cl): radius decreases.**
Proton number ↑, inner electrons constant → Zeff ↑ → orbital pulled inward → stronger nucleus–valence attraction → smaller radius.

| Period 2 | Li | Be | B | C | N | O | F |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Zeff | +1 | +2 | +3 | +4 | +5 | +6 | +7 |
| Radius (pm) | 123 | 112 | 80 | 77 | 74 | 73 | 72 |

| Period 3 | Na | Mg | Al | Si | P | S | Cl |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Zeff | +1 | +2 | +3 | +4 | +5 | +6 | +7 |
| Radius (pm) | 156 | 136 | 125 | 117 | 110 | 104 | 99 |

**First-row transition metals**: only slight/gradual variation in radius across the row (d-electrons shield each other similarly to nuclear charge increase).

**Down a group (e.g. Li→Na→K→Rb; F→Cl→Br→I): radius increases.**
Principal quantum number (n) ↑ → more inner shells → shielding ↑ → attraction on valence e⁻ weakens → radius increases.

| Group 1 | Li | Na | K | Rb | | Group 17 | F | Cl | Br | I |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Radius (pm) | 167 | 190 | 243 | 265 | | Radius (pm) | 42 | 79 | 94 | 115 |

**Example 6.7**: N (Zeff=+5) vs F (Zeff=+7) — N has the larger atom because its lower Zeff means weaker nucleus–valence attraction.

## 4. Ionic Radius

- **Cation < parent atom**: losing valence e⁻ reduces electron–electron repulsion, so remaining electrons are pulled in closer.
- **Anion > parent atom**: gaining valence e⁻ increases repulsion, pushing electrons further out.

![Comparison of atomic radii and ionic radii across the periodic table, shown as proportionally-sized circles](https://chem.libretexts.org/@api/deki/files/129741/imageedit_25_8555107656.jpg?revision=1)
*Figure 2: Atomic radii (outer, coloured circles) vs. ionic radii (inner, grey circles) — cations are visibly smaller and anions visibly larger than their parent atom. Ionic radius data from R. D. Shannon, "Revised effective ionic radii and systematic studies of interatomic distances in halides and chalcogenides," Acta Crystallographica A32 (1976): 751–767, via [Chemistry LibreTexts — "7.3: Sizes of Atoms and Ions"](https://chem.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/General_Chemistry/Map:_Chemistry_-_The_Central_Science_(Brown_et_al.)/07:_Periodic_Properties_of_the_Elements/7.03:_Sizes_of_Atoms_and_Ions), licensed [CC BY-NC-SA 3.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-sa/3.0/).*

| Atom→Cation | e⁻ | Proton no. | Atomic r (nm) | Ionic r (nm) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Na→Na⁺ | 11→10 | 11 | 0.156 | 0.095 |
| Mg→Mg²⁺ | 12→10 | 12 | 0.136 | 0.065 |
| Al→Al³⁺ | 13→10 | 13 | 0.125 | 0.050 |

| Atom→Anion | e⁻ | Proton no. | Atomic r (nm) | Ionic r (nm) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P→P³⁻ | 15→18 | 15 | 0.110 | 0.212 |
| S→S²⁻ | 16→18 | 16 | 0.104 | 0.184 |
| Cl→Cl⁻ | 17→18 | 17 | 0.099 | 0.181 |

**Example 6.8**: X (Z=11) → X⁺: ionic radius < atomic radius. Y (Z=15) → Y³⁻: ionic radius > atomic radius.

### Isoelectronic species
Same number of electrons & same electron configuration, but different Zeff. Higher Zeff → stronger attraction → smaller radius.

| Species | O²⁻ | F⁻ | Ne | Na⁺ | Mg²⁺ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Radius (nm) | 0.140 | 0.136 | 0.112 | 0.095 | 0.065 |
| Zeff | +6 | +7 | +8 | +9 | +10 |

**Example 6.9**: Li⁺, Be²⁺, B³⁺ (isoelectronic) → radius order Li⁺ > Be²⁺ > B³⁺ (Zeff increases while inner e⁻ constant).

**Across Period 2/3 ionic radius pattern**: cations shrink (Li⁺→B³⁺, Na⁺→Al³⁺), then a **drastic jump up** at the cation→anion switch (B³⁺→N³⁻, Al³⁺→P³⁻ — an extra shell appears), then anions shrink again (N³⁻→F⁻, P³⁻→Cl⁻).

| Period 2 cations | Li⁺ | Be²⁺ | B³⁺ | | Period 3 cations | Na⁺ | Mg²⁺ | Al³⁺ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ionic r (nm) | 60 | 31 | 20 | | Ionic r (nm) | 95 | 65 | 50 |

| B³⁺→N³⁻ | e⁻ | Ionic r (nm) | | Al³⁺→P³⁻ | e⁻ | Ionic r (nm) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| B³⁺ | 2 | 20 | | Al³⁺ | 10 | 50 |
| N³⁻ | 10 | 171 | | P³⁻ | 18 | 212 |

| Period 2 anions | N³⁻ | O²⁻ | F⁻ | | Period 3 anions | P³⁻ | S²⁻ | Cl⁻ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Zeff | +5 | +6 | +7 | | Zeff | +5 | +6 | +7 |
| Ionic r (nm) | 171 | 140 | 136 | | Ionic r (nm) | 212 | 184 | 181 |

**Example 6.10**: across Period 3 (Na⁺→Cl⁻): cations shrink Na⁺→Al³⁺, jump up Al³⁺→P³⁻, then anions shrink P³⁻→Cl⁻ (same Zeff/isoelectronic logic as above).

## 5. Ionisation Energy (IE)

**Definition**: energy needed to remove an electron attracted to the nucleus; always endothermic (+ve). Lower IE = electron more easily removed.

- **IE1**: X(g) + IE1 → X⁺(g) + e⁻. E.g. Mg(g) + 738 kJ/mol → Mg⁺(g) + e⁻
- **IE2**: X⁺(g) + IE2 → X²⁺(g) + e⁻. E.g. Mg⁺(g) + 1451 kJ/mol → Mg²⁺(g) + e⁻ (lower IE = easier to form that cation)

**Factors affecting IE**:
1. Effective nuclear charge — higher Zeff → stronger attraction → harder to remove e⁻ → higher IE
2. Shielding effect — higher shielding → weaker attraction → easier to remove e⁻ → lower IE
3. Atomic radius — larger radius → weaker attraction on valence e⁻ → lower IE

**Across a period: IE1 increases.** Zeff ↑ (inner e⁻ constant) → stronger attraction, smaller radius → harder to remove e⁻.

**Down a group: IE1 decreases.** n ↑ → shielding ↑ → weaker attraction, larger radius → easier to remove e⁻.

**Anomalies in the IE1 trend** (Period 2 & 3 behave alike):
- **Be > B** (and **Mg > Al**): Be/Mg has a fully-filled ns² (extra stable) vs B/Al's partially-filled np¹ — more energy needed to break the stable filled sub-shell.
- **N/O and P/S** show a similar anomaly (fully/half-filled p-subshell stability): the 1st IE dips between them despite the general increasing trend.

**Successive ionisation energies → deduce electron configuration & PT position.**
Removing electrons gets progressively harder (increasing +ve charge on the ion), so IE1 < IE2 < IE3... A **drastic jump** between successive IEs marks the boundary of a shell (inner, stable noble-gas-like electrons are much harder to remove) — the position of the jump reveals the number of valence electrons.

Example (Na): IE1 = 496, IE2 = 4560 kJ/mol → drastic jump from IE1→IE2 shows Na has only **1 valence electron** (confirmed by the "log Iₙ vs. electrons removed" plot for Na, which jumps sharply after the 1st electron and again after the 9th).

**Example 6.11**: Period 2 elements X (IE: 899, 1760, 14850, 21000) and Y (IE: 801, 2430, 3660, 25000) kJ/mol — X has 2 valence e⁻ (jump after IE2), Y has 3 valence e⁻ (jump after IE3); write configs; X's IE1 > Y's IE1 because X has higher Zeff / smaller radius.

**Example 6.12**: Element Z, Period 3 — successive IE plot shows a drastic jump at the **5th** ionisation energy → Z has **4 valence electrons**, Group 14, p-block, configuration 1s²2s²2p⁶3s²3p² (i.e. silicon).

## 6. Electronegativity (EN)

**Definition**: the relative tendency of a bonded atom to attract electrons toward itself. Measured on the **Pauling scale**. Fluorine is the most electronegative (EN = 4.0); Cs and Fr are the least (EN ≈ 0.7).

**Across a period: EN increases** (same reasoning as atomic radius/IE — Zeff ↑, radius contracts, bonding electrons pulled in more strongly).

| Period 2 | Li | Be | B | C | N | O | F |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| EN | 1.0 | 1.5 | 2.0 | 2.5 | 3.0 | 3.5 | 4.0 |

| Period 3 | Na | Mg | Al | Si | P | S | Cl |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| EN | 0.9 | 1.2 | 1.5 | 1.8 | 2.1 | 2.5 | 3.0 |

**Example 6.13**: F (EN 4.0) > N (EN 3.0) — higher Zeff in F, smaller radius, stronger pull on bonding electrons.

## 7. Acid–Base Character of Period 3 Oxides

General rule: more metallic elements → more basic oxides; more metallic down a group; less metallic (more non-metallic) across a period → oxides become more acidic.

| Element | Na | Mg | Al | Si | P | S | Cl |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Oxide | Na₂O | MgO | Al₂O₃ | SiO₂ | P₄O₆/P₄O₁₀ | SO₂/SO₃ | Cl₂O/ClO₂/Cl₂O₇ |
| Character | Basic | Basic | Amphoteric | Acidic | Acidic | Acidic | Acidic |
| Solubility | Soluble | Insoluble | — | Soluble | — | — | — |
| With acid | Reacts | — | — | No reaction | — | — | — |
| With base | No reaction | — | Reacts | — | — | — | — |

**Basic oxides (Na, Mg)** react with acid → salt + water:
- Na₂O(s) + 2HCl(aq) → 2NaCl(aq) + H₂O(l)
- MgO(s) + 2HCl(aq) → 2MgCl₂(aq) + H₂O(l) *(coefficient should balance to MgCl₂, not NaCl as shown on the slide)*

**Acidic oxides (Si, P, S, Cl)** react with base → salt + water:
- SiO₂(s) + 2NaOH(aq) → Na₂SiO₃(aq) + H₂O(l)
- P₄O₁₀(s) + 12NaOH(aq) → 4Na₃PO₄(aq) + 6H₂O(l) *(equation should balance to 6H₂O, not 1H₂O as shown on the slide)*

**Amphoteric oxide (Al)** reacts with both acid and base:
- Al₂O₃(s) + 6HCl(aq) → 2AlCl₃(aq) + 3H₂O(l)
- Al₂O₃(s) + 2NaOH(aq) → 2NaAlO₂(aq) + H₂O(l) *(slide shows "6NaOH → 6NaAlO₂ + 3H₂O" with only 1 Al₂O₃, which doesn't balance Al — the correctly balanced ratio is 1 : 2 : 2 : 1 as shown here)*

## Quick Revision Table

| Property | Across a period (L→R) | Down a group |
|---|---|---|
| Zeff | Increases | Increases (but shielding grows faster) |
| Atomic radius | Decreases | Increases |
| Ionic radius (isoelectronic series) | Decreases as Zeff increases | Increases |
| 1st Ionisation Energy | Increases (with Be/Mg & N/P anomalies) | Decreases |
| Electronegativity | Increases | Decreases |
